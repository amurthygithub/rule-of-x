The Rule of 40 (growth plus margin of at least 40%) is a software benchmark. The frontier of attainable growth-margin pairs is concave when capacity leads revenue, and Rule-of-40 and Rule-of-X lines are price lines, of equal score, not equal value, whose tangency with it gives the maximal score: the margin intercept, plus market growth net of the capital it requires, plus a transient growth surplus drawn from a finite budget of share headroom in one market. Under a verification hypothesis, a value-maximizing firm weights growth at the marginal value of revenue, above screening weights of 2 to 3 when its revenue multiple is well above 3. In first-reported SEC filings of 284 software firms in 2010-25, the 95th percentile of the score fell between 2016-19 and 2023-25 by 11.9 points on the GAAP basis (significant at 10% under three of four familywise corrections) and 12.6 on free cash flow after stock-based compensation (not significant), while fewer firms grew fast. At the top, a point of growth came with about a fifth of a point less margin across firms in 2010-25 (-0.60 in 2020-25, 0-40% growth, GAAP); the data do not identify the weight firms act on. At central full costs and fixed prices, complete automation keeps a mature asset-light firm's cash score below 100 unless its market grows faster than 32.5% a year, with compute at 6% of revenue (28 to 78% over its scenario range; 36 or 39% if half or 70% of savings pass to customers). On a central scenario, not a forecast, a top-tail firm realizes 41% of its program's benefits by 2036 and has a durable score of 58 (53 net of the transition dip; anchor-only interval 54 to 62; 55 to 65 by selling-spend basis); the program repays its transition costs after 7.5 years. Acquisitions and interest distort scores. Under free entry into a homogeneous market, automation raises only the scores of firms with a scarce input, up to the full-cost ceiling.
