The Rule of 40 (revenue growth plus profit margin of at least 40%) is a software benchmark. The frontier of attainable growth-margin pairs is concave when capacity leads revenue, and Rule-of-40 and Rule-of-X lines are price lines, of equal score, not equal value, whose tangency with it gives the maximal score: the margin intercept, plus market growth net of the capital it requires, plus a transient growth surplus drawn, within one market, from a finite budget of share headroom. Under a verification hypothesis, a value-maximizing firm weights growth at the marginal value of revenue, above screening weights of two to three when its revenue multiple is well above three. In first-reported SEC filings of 292 software firms in 2010-25, the 95th percentile of the score fell between 2016-19 and 2023-25 by 11.9 points on the GAAP basis (significant at 10% after familywise correction under most procedures) and 12.7 on free cash flow after stock-based compensation (not significant), while fewer firms grew fast. At the top, a point of growth came with about a fifth of a point less margin across firms over 2010-25 (-0.60 in 2020-25, 0-40% growth, GAAP); the data do not identify the weight firms act on. At full cost and fixed prices, complete automation keeps a mature asset-light firm's cash score below 100 unless its market grows faster than 32.5% a year, with compute at 6% of revenue (28 to 78% over its credible range; 36 or 38% if half or 70% of savings pass to customers). On a central scenario, not a forecast, a top-tail firm realizes 41% of its program's benefits by 2036 and scores 59 [54, 62] (55 to 65 as none or all selling spend buys growth); the program repays its transition costs after 7.5 years. Acquisitions and interest distort the score. Under free entry into a homogeneous market, automation's savings raise only the scores of firms with a scarce input, up to the full-cost ceiling.
