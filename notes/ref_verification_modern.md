Checked on 2026-10-29. As in the earlier rounds, direct API access (Crossref, doi.org, export.arxiv.org, NBER, IDEAS, NIST via curl) was blocked by the session proxy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT; confirmed again with `__agentproxy/status`), so every item was verified with web search and by fetching a landing page: NBER paper or chapter pages, RePEc/IDEAS/EconPapers, arXiv abstract pages, publisher or proceedings pages (INFORMS DOI page, NeurIPS and ICLR proceedings, AAAI OJS, Nature Index DOI records), NIST CSRC and nist.gov publication records, the Federal Register (govinfo and public inspection), whitehouse.gov and the White House archive, the UK NCSC, the European Parliament, the Council of the EU and the European Commission. Where a publisher page gave only the DOI and title, volume and pages were taken from an institutional research-information record (Pure) and the source is named. DOIs marked "via RePEc handle" come from the hdl.handle.net link on the RePEc record.

Status key, as in the earlier logs: **verified** = every field in the BibTeX entry was confirmed on a page listed below (fields that could not be confirmed were left out). **corrected** = the item exists but its venue, year, title or status differs from the task brief or from common citation; the BibTeX holds the corrected version. **dropped** = not included.

What-it-shows column: one sentence, from the abstract or summary on the page named.

**Totals.** 81 new entries in `part2/refs_modern.bib`: 75 verified, 6 corrected. 6 items dropped. No key duplicates a key in `refs.bib` or `part2/refs_part2.bib` (checked by script). 15 keys already in those files were re-checked or reused (section R). The test build (pdflatex + bibtex with all three bib files and plainnat, in the session scratchpad) produced no BibTeX warnings and no undefined citations; see the end of this log.

## Notes for the authors

1. **NIST IR 8547 is final (October 2026).** Cite moody2026transition, not the November 2024 draft. All quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms are deprecated after 2030 and disallowed after 2035. For firms that sell to the US government, EO 14412 adds a proposed FAR requirement to comply with FIPS, including PQC, by 31 December 2030; federal high-value and high-impact systems must use PQC for key establishment by end-2030 and signatures by end-2031.
2. **PQC cost evidence is public-sector only.** The one quantified estimate is OMB's $7.1 billion (2024 dollars, 2025-2035, federal civilian prioritized systems, rough order of magnitude). NCSC gives effort durations (2-3 years for discovery and planning, 2-3 years for early migration) but no cost. No peer-reviewed or credible firm-level migration cost estimate was found, so the engine's PQC cost parameters must be marked assumed, with the inventory scaled by systems and the deadline from the regime.
3. **Quantum advantage for business optimization is unproven.** No verified source shows a practical advantage for optimization. Error correction has crossed the surface-code threshold (Google 2025) and a verifiable physics-simulation advantage is claimed (Google 2025, OTOC), while claimed simulation advantages have been followed by classical simulations (IBM 2023 by Tindall et al. 2024; D-Wave 2025 by Mauron and Carleo 2025). The quantum scenario flag should change only the PQC cost by default.
4. **EU AI Act timing changed.** Regulation 2026/1744 moves high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I). The engine's regulatory-class calendar should use these dates.
5. **Published versions.** Bick, Blandin and Deming is now Management Science (2026); Autor and Thompson is JEEA (2025); Acemoglu and Restrepo's rent dissipation paper is QJE (2026). Agarwal et al., Eisfeldt et al., Hampole et al. and Shahidi et al. remain working papers.
