## 1. Fixes, old to new, with citations

### IB-4 and the Fable finance referee: financing was charged twice

1. **Basis of the cash margin (Remark 3).**
   - Old: "Let m^cf be a cash margin that excludes the cash paid for acquisitions (operating cash flow over revenue, for instance)", with the hedge "If operating cash flow is stated after interest paid, the annuity at the cost of capital charges financing twice."
   - New: m^cf is the margin before acquisitions and before financing, (OCF + (1 − τ) interest paid)/S. Under U.S. GAAP interest paid is an operating cash flow (ASC 230-10-45-17(d); Deloitte, Roadmap: Comparing IFRS and U.S. GAAP, §4.3). A cost-of-capital charge set against OCF as reported charges the debt-financed share twice: "charge interest or a cost of capital on the same capital, not both".
   - Citation: the company's statements are "prepared in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles" (6-K Ex. 99.2, Note 1; F-1 and 424B4: "prepared in U.S. dollars in accordance with GAAP"). The interim cash-flow statement reports "Interests paid" of $49.7M (H1 2025) and $161.2M (H1 2026) within operating activities.
2. **Tax on the add-back.** τ = 24%, Italy's IRES rate. Interest is excluded from the IRAP base (PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Italy, last reviewed 13 July 2026), so 24% is the largest saving. τ = 0 is the other end.
   - Interest paid for 2024 and 2025 is not in the documents read, so interest expense stands in for it. Interest paid was 90.4% of interest expense in H1 2025 and 79.7% in H1 2026 (6-K Ex. 99.1 and 99.2), so the add-back is if anything too large.
3. **Conventions made consistent and stated (Table 9).**
   - Old: the rows mixed bases. The GAAP score of 115.9 is before interest; the "uncharged, cash" score of 116.9 is after interest paid.
   - New: every row says which it is.
     - GAAP before interest 115.9; GAAP after interest 105.0.
     - Cash after interest paid (OCF as reported) 116.9.
     - Cash before interest 125.2 at τ = 24% (127.8 at τ = 0).
     - Charged-as-capital rows before interest; fully charged before interest.
4. **The middle case, recomputed across conventions.**
   - Old: 84.4 to 92.8, a level annuity on year-end capital set against OCF after interest.
   - New: 87 to 114 for 2025. This spans four charges (level annuity or user cost (r + d) on decayed capital, each on year-end or time-weighted capital), NRR 91% to 95%, τ of 0 or 24%, and second-half 2025 outlays dated between 1 October and Vimeo's close on 24 November.
   - Set against OCF after interest, the same charges give 79 to 103. The double charge is exactly (1 − τ)c_fin: 8.3 points of 2025 revenue at 24% (the referee estimated about 8) and 10.9 at 0.
   - The panel's 78 to 109 (B5) mixed double-charged rows (levered OCF) with consistent ones and used its own capital timing. Its unlevered rows, 85.8 to 108.8, are of the same kind as my 87 to 114; the differences come from the timing of 2025 outlays, which I take by quarter and half-year from the 6-K comparatives.
   - Precedent for the user cost: Hall and Jorgenson (1967), and P1's own Remark 4(c), which treats growth capital that depreciates.
5. **Capital spending.**
   - Old: "The net investing outflow includes capital spending and other items that our extract does not separate, which makes both charged scores too low."
   - New: in H1 2025 and H1 2026, acquisitions net of cash acquired were 99.9% and 99.8% of the net investing outflow, and purchases of PP&E 0.05% and 0.2% ($4.0M against $2,286M in H1 2026) (6-K Ex. 99.2).
6. **Return on acquisition capital (new).** Operating cash flow before interest over the time-weighted capital deployed in acquisitions since 2023 was 22% to 28% in 2025. After economic depreciation at d = −ln NRR it was 14% to 22%, against a cost of capital of 8.93%; at τ = 24% the figures are 22.3–25.8% and 13.6–19.8%.
   - Stated biases: the cash flow of businesses bought before 2023 is counted without their price, which overstates the return. Expensed transaction and reorganization costs stay in the cash flow, which understates it.
   - The panel's 20.4/20.7% and 11.3/15.3% used a different capital timing.

### Fable theory F21: the base's zero-spend growth

1. **P1 Proposition 14(d).**
   - Old: "the existing base contributes (S_sub/S)(NRR − 1) to annual organic growth. Its zero-spend growth is G_i = ln NRR".
   - New: with the subscription share S_sub/S and the rest of revenue changing by γ_ns a year without spend (shares at the start of the year), the base contributes a = (S_sub/S)(NRR − 1) + (1 − S_sub/S)γ_ns to annual organic growth.
   - Its continuously compounded zero-spend growth is G_i = ln(1 + a). That equals ln NRR if all revenue is subscription revenue, and lies in [ln NRR, 0] if NRR < 1 and γ_ns ∈ [NRR − 1, 0]. The annual rate is converted with ln(1 + r), as the referee asked.
2. **P1 Proposition 14(f).** Holding g requires g^acq = g + d^org, with d^org := −g^org. Without new customers d^org = −G_i, which is −ln NRR only when all revenue is subscription revenue. The proofs of (d) and (f) are rewritten.
3. **P2 prop:acq-limits(a) (treadmill).**
   - Old: "Standing still requires acquired growth −g^org, which is −ln NRR when no new customers arrive".
   - New: "Holding growth g requires acquired growth g + d^org … at a cash cost of k^acq(g + d^org) … When no new customers arrive, d^org = −G_i …, which is −ln NRR when all revenue is subscription revenue; standing still (g = 0) then requires acquired growth −G_i." The proof is rewritten.
4. **P2 Grounding (NRR 91–95%, continuously compounded).**
   - Organic decay with no new customers and all revenue on subscription: 5.1% to 9.4% a year (5% to 9% annual).
   - Standing still: $67M to $123M of run-rate a year, at a cash cost of 8.9% to 30.5% of revenue at 1.7–3.2 times revenue.
   - Holding 2025's growth (66.6% continuously compounded): 71.7% to 76.0% of revenue a year, at 124% to 246% of revenue in cash.
   - At the company's Q2 organic growth of 3% (3.0% continuously compounded): 63.7%. Self-financed g^max there is 9% to 20%, against 1% to 12% at 2025's NRR with no new customers.
5. **P2 prop:acq-retention(b), its proof and the case calibration.** Business-level ln NRR is the zero-spend growth of subscription revenue; it is G_i only for an all-subscription business. δ^G is the change in G_i.
6. **P1 Definition 3 (Fable finance minor point).**
   - The continuously compounded rates are g^org = ln(S^cn_t/S_{t−1}) and g^acq = ln(1 + S^ac_t/S^cn_t). They add to ln(S_t/S_{t−1}).
   - The simple rates add as practitioners compute them.
   - C^acq is a share of current revenue.

### The growth weight (decision D-1)

1. **P1 (new sentence, % XBOUNDS).** Charged in full, acquired growth raises R^A_X if and only if X > k^acq.
   - At X = 2.5 this holds for Eventbrite (1.7) and AOL (2.3) but not Vimeo (3.2); at X = 5 and 9 it holds for all three.
   - The sign flips at X = k^acq, between 1.7 and 3.2.
   - Spread as an annuity or a user cost (at most 0.59 points per point), acquired growth raises the score at every placeholder.
2. **P2 replace example (% XBOUNDS).**
   - Old: "1.69% at a growth weight of one but only 0.21% at a public firm's weight of eight".
   - New: break-even loss G^spd/(X^o S) = 1.69% at X = 1, and 0.67%, 0.34% and 0.19% at X^o = 2.5, 5 and 9.
   - It scales as 1/X^o and never changes sign.
3. **No other statement in these files uses X = 7–13, 8 or 9.** The P1 "X = 1" statements are the Rule of 40 itself. prop:acq-pc(d) and prop:acq-retention(d) are ratios that do not depend on X.
