## Capital allocation and financing (shareholder letter and MD&A)

- **Return hurdles:** internal-rate-of-return hurdles of 65% levered and 25% unlevered, for acquisitions completed from 2023 through Q1 2026.
- **IRR method:**
  - five years of free cash flow attributable to the acquired business, plus a terminal value;
  - recent deals: terminal value from growth in years 6 to 10 and a WACC;
  - earlier deals: an EV/EBITDA multiple at year 5;
  - assumed acquisition debt: the lower of 85% of enterprise value and the debt that the business's projected free cash flow could repay within five years.
- **Capital deployed in acquisitions:** $2.01 billion in Q1 2026, against $194 million in all of 2023. More than 200 targets were evaluated in depth in 2025.
- **Capital policy:** almost all available capital is to go to acquisitions "for many years to come". Acquired businesses are never sold: the company says it invests on expected returns "assuming we'll never sell the acquired business".
- **Debt:**
  - term loan A since 2017, term loan B first in 2025, no bonds;
  - leverage-ratio covenant of at most 4.00; the ratio was 2.24 at end-2025 and 2.19 at end-Q1 2026.
- **Equity:** $549 million of primary equity raised in total, 99% of it since 2023; dilution averaged 6.1% a year from 2023 to Q1 2026.
- **Operating cash flow growth:** 246% in 2024, 42% in 2025 and 254% in Q1 2026; CAGR 122% from 2023 to 2025.
- **Role models cited:** Henry Singleton (Teledyne), Tom Murphy (Capital Cities), Broadcom, Danaher and TransDigm.
- **Playbook:** "acquire, transform and optimize, then reinvest", essentially unchanged since 2013. What has changed is the company's understanding of execution, which rests on people, proprietary technologies and proprietary data (the "Platform").

## Transformation practice

- **Restructuring:** the company says it reorganizes "at least with each acquisition" (workforce reductions, restructurings and reorganizations). Its risk factors list loss of institutional knowledge, disruption, morale and reputation.
- **Starting a turnaround:** it typically restructures significantly, "often transitioning to a much smaller, more talent-dense organization", and eliminates rules and processes ("startup mode").
- **Examples given:**
  - Remini (asset deal, no team transferred; about 30 FTE Spooners allocated; codebase rewritten in full);
  - Evernote (organization rearchitected, technology modernized);
  - StreamYard (leaner organization).
- **Current focus:** transforming AOL, Eventbrite and Vimeo.
- **Spooners (the core team):** 621 FTE at end-Q1 2026, 27% of all FTE team members (implying about 2,300 FTE in total). 42% are in technical roles, and they are "transferred between businesses on short notice".
- **Hiring:** about 800,000 applications and 286 hires in 2025. The Talent team has 55 FTE Spooners, 9% of Spooners.
- **Unions:** "to our knowledge, none of our employees are currently represented" by a union (truncated in the extract; confirm the exact wording).
- **AOL as acquired:** about 214 direct employees at 31 Dec 2025, excluding shared services from the parent. Acquisition closed 2 January 2026.

## AI

- **AI-authored code:** pull requests authored or co-authored by AI rose from under 10% in Q1 2025 to over 90% by end-Q1 2026; about 70% were authored by AI alone.
- **Revenue per FTE Spooner:** $1.12 million in 2023, $2.57 million in 2025, and $0.97 million in Q1 2026 (a single quarter).
  - The quarterly figure divides revenue by Spooners at quarter-end; the annual figure divides by the average of quarter-end counts. The two are not comparable.
  - The figures count Spooners only, not all staff.
- **The company's own claims:** AI has been used "for several years" in products, marketing and monetization, and productivity. AI helped "in part". AI should make the model more scalable ("accomplish more with fewer people").

## Customers and retention

- **Net revenue retention:**
  - Portfolio: 93% (2023), 91% (2024), 95% (2025) and 94% (Q1 2026).
  - Averaged over Q1 2023 to Q1 2026: AOL 95%, Evernote 99%, Remini 87%, StreamYard 91%.
  - Definition: subscription revenue in a quarter from customers acquired at least a year earlier, divided by subscription revenue in the same quarter a year earlier.
  - Net revenue retention below 100% means the existing subscription base shrinks each year, so growth at portfolio level comes mainly from acquisitions (the F-1 says portfolio changes are "primarily driven by new acquisitions").
- **Tenure:** in Q1 2026, 48% of subscription revenue came from customers with tenure of at least five years and 28% from at least ten. Revenue-weighted average tenure was 8.0 years.
- **New customers:** organic channels brought 79% (2023), 76% (2024), 79% (2025) and 83% (Q1 2026) of revenue from new customers.
- **Geography:** 65% of revenue from North America and 21% from Europe (period as stated in the F-1).
- **Users:** more than 500 million monthly active users and more than 9 million monthly paying customers (March 2026). This figure is from secondary sources and has not been rechecked in the F-1.

## Controls and reporting

- **Material weaknesses:** the company has identified material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting. It warns that integrating acquisitions may leave them unremediated and create new ones.
- **No SOX 404 assessment yet:** "neither we nor an independent registered public accounting firm have performed an evaluation" of ICFR under SOX.
- **Restatement:** the Q1 2026 cash flows are restated.
- **Governance:** foreign private issuer; dual-class shares, with the four founders holding class A.

### Tax rate and accounting standards (practitioner sources)
- **Italy:** "24% for IRES" and "3.9% for IRAP"; for sales and manufacturing companies "interest income and expense ... are excluded for the purposes of the IRAP taxable base" (PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Italy, Taxes on corporate income, last reviewed 13 July 2026). So interest saves tax at the IRES rate of 24% at most.
- **ASC 230-10:** under U.S. GAAP "Interest paid and received should be classified as operating activities" (Deloitte, Roadmap: Comparing IFRS Accounting Standards and U.S. GAAP, section 4.3).
- **ASC 805-10-25-23:** "The acquirer shall account for acquisition-related costs as expenses in the periods in which the costs are incurred and the services are received, with one exception" (debt and equity issuance costs) (Deloitte, Roadmap: Business Combinations, section 5.4).
