## 2. Statements checked against these sources (text locations)

- **ASC 350-40 and ASU 2025-06** (sec:acct-standards, tab:acct-map, prop:acct-path(e)): all as in the table above.
  - The statement that before ASU 2025-06 capitalization occurs only in the application development stage is from the Codification as it stood. The ASU confirms the stage model exists ("prescriptive and sequential software development stages"); the stage names were not read in a primary source.
- **Hosted arrangements:** ASU 2018-15 paragraphs as above. That capitalized internal-use software is an investing outflow is consistent with the proposal described by KPMG ("shown apart from other investing outflows"); ASC 230 was not read.
- **ASC 420, 712, 360, 718 and 740:** as above.
- **Tax:** §174A, §174, Rev. Proc. 2025-28, Notice 2023-63 and Notice 2026-11 as above. ψ_T, the 1/30 first-year fraction and the 9.8-cent penalty are computed in the verify script (checks 3a to 3e).
- **SOX, Reg. S-K, Rule 12b-2 and the SEC guidance and FAQ:** as above.
  - "Relief ... not, as far as we found, for a system implementation" is a negative finding: no FAQ or rule granting relief for system changes was found; FAQ Q7 treats a new system as a change that may require disclosure.
- **AS 2201, AS 1105 and the PCAOB staff publications:** as above.
  - "We found no PCAOB standard or staff guidance on AI-performed controls as of October 2026" rests on the PCAOB staff-publications page (only the 2024 GenAI and data/reports Spotlights in 2024 to 2026) and the standards list.
  - A practitioner article (Fieldguide, 6 August 2026) states: "The PCAOB has not issued a single rule written specifically for AI." This is a negative finding, so it could be overtaken.
- **COSO 2013 and 2026:** as above.
- **Judgments, flagged in the text:**
  - mapping of model training, fine-tuning, prompt and agent development, labeling and evaluation suites;
  - that a model-run control fails the benchmarking factors of AS 2201.B31;
  - T_op as a minimum operating period;
  - the example shares u = 40% and data conversion = 5/8 of integration (rebuilt example of 29 Oct: data conversion = 1/4 of integration; other one-time costs expensed, by judgment except training, 25-4).
- **Scope, ITGCs and timeliness (sec:acct-scope, added 29 Oct; re-read at the sources that day):**
  - SEC Release 33-8810, Section II.A.1.d, verbatim: "For purposes of the evaluation of ICFR, management only needs to evaluate those IT general controls that are necessary for the proper and consistent operation of other controls designed to adequately address financial reporting risks." Also verbatim: "it is unnecessary to evaluate IT general controls that primarily pertain to efficiency or effectiveness of a company's operations, but which are not relevant to addressing financial reporting risks"; example areas "program development, program changes, computer operations, and access to programs and data" (footnote 37 says they need not all apply). The text quotes the first sentence and paraphrases the others without change of meaning.
  - AS 2201.36, verbatim: "The auditor also should understand how IT affects the company's flow of transactions." Note to .36: "The identification of risks and controls within IT is not a separate evaluation. Instead, it is an integral part of the top-down approach ..." The text cites the Note.
  - AS 2201.47, bullets read verbatim: "The degree to which the control relies on the effectiveness of other controls (e.g., the control environment or information technology general controls)"; "an automated control would generally be expected to be lower risk if relevant information technology general controls are effective". The text quotes the last clause.
  - AS 2201.B29 (benchmarking conditional on effective general controls over program changes, access to programs and computer operations) and .B30 (automated application controls may depend on related files, tables, data and parameters; interest-rate-table example). That prompts, retrieval corpora, thresholds and tool permissions are such parameters is flagged as our judgment.
  - COSO 2026 GenAI guidance (mirror copy of the COSO PDF, printed page numbers): p. 6 does not repeat established ITGC guidance; p. 11 "vendor-driven changes, which may require specific notification obligations or independent verification"; p. 13 models and configurations as configuration items "subject to access control, change management, and other IT operations controls", and reliance cases held to "the same evidence standards expected for ICFR"; p. 14 "periodically retest to confirm ongoing reliability", no frequency. Negative findings (read for, not found): no treatment of AI outages, business continuity or manual fallback; service accounts and non-human identities not addressed as such; no discussion of how auditors rely on AI-performed controls; PCAOB standards not discussed (only in an author biography). Negative findings could be overtaken by later COSO material.
  - The four-channel scope definition is the author's scoping principle (processes, systems, integrations and policies that affect the timeliness, accuracy and completeness of financial reporting), formalized; the text says so. Unit costs of the ITGC line and the timeliness parameters are illustrative.

## 4. Corrections made during drafting (for the protocol appendix, spec 23.3)

1. Deloitte Example 2 was first summarized as expensing the training needed to prove the novel function. On re-reading, Deloitte capitalizes that training once coding and testing have resolved the uncertainty. The text was corrected.
2. "Changes in ICFR during the fourth quarter" was corrected to Item 308(c)'s "last fiscal quarter (the fourth quarter in the annual report)". "Material changes" was replaced by the rule's words.
3. AS 2201.53 was first paraphrased as "only if", which turns a sufficient condition into a necessary one. It was corrected to "need not be tested if".
4. "Capitalization ceases when ..." was restored to the standard's "no later than when ...".
5. A second SEC release number for Release 33-8810 (34-55929) was removed as unverified.
6. A comparative statement in the a_min example ("with fewer opportunities ... AI costs more") had the wrong direction. Check 5i showed that more opportunities, or a higher common-mode rate, make the all-AI design costlier. The text was corrected.
7. The tax paragraph first said the 100% first-year allowance "can be elected down". The final text states only the 100% allowance from Notice 2026-11; the 40% election (IR-2026-06) is recorded in the bibliography note and the engine notes.
8. The first rebuild against the new invoice JSON matched the alias `varpi` to an activity-level success probability (0.997) instead of the transition's `varpi_p` (0.639). Check 4f (reproduce the core's NPV) caught it, and the alias order was fixed.
9. The prior-law tax example first amortized all of F as software development. Data conversion and training are excluded (Notice 2023-63), and the other one-time costs are not development; the example now amortizes only the $2.12 million of software development.
10. First drafts of the scope addition used λ, K and 𝒢 for local quantities. These clash with Part I's growth parameter, the capital K_j and Part I's set of growth rates, so they became ν_cw, n_up and 𝒰*_AI.
