### 2.3 METR time-horizon data (capability path)
- Source: github.com/METR/eval-analysis-public, cloned 29 Oct 2026, commit 52cb829c7a2efb2d659285c4b1768d191d97f8d2 (2026-03-05).
- License: **not stated**. The README says "See LICENSE file for details", but no LICENSE file exists at this commit. Used here for analysis only; the lead author may want to confirm terms with METR before redistributing the run data (we do not redistribute it; only fitted horizons are written).
- Files and sha256:
  - `reports/time-horizon-1-1/data/raw/runs.jsonl`: 609f904f4b6ae32129388da89d036e00bac511ad94223d2be1e58fc2b45b55cd (24,008 runs, 20 agents plus human baselines, HCAST, SWAA, RE-Bench)
  - `reports/time-horizon-1-0/data/raw/runs.jsonl`: f489d11728909937dad99ba1ed2d7c40efe12a766891e67dbc7c2486a3311236
  - `data/external/release_dates.yaml`: 317b92915df5bf935908567a857116bcf6f0c7686ef8b0840897ed04a29232bc
- Paper: Kwa et al., "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks", arXiv:2503.14499 (v3, 25 Feb 2026, read at arxiv.org/html/2503.14499v3; NeurIPS 2025 per the literature agent): 207 days doubling (95% CI 166-240) for 2019-2025; 2024-onward models 143 days; 80% horizon doubling 204 days; frontier o3 about 110 minutes in the original version.
- Method (`06_capability.py`), replicating METR's code in the same repository: weighted logistic regression of `score_binarized` on log2(human minutes) per agent (weights 1 / (runs in task x sqrt(tasks in family)), normalised; sklearn C = 1e5); p-horizon = 2^((logit(p) - intercept)/coefficient); SOTA agents = those whose p50 is at least the best p50 released on or before their date; OLS of ln(p50) on release date; doubling = ln 2 / slope. CIs: 200 cluster-bootstrap draws over task families (METR's own bootstrap is hierarchical, so CIs differ).
- Results (`_build/capability.json`, `capability_metr_th11.csv`):
  - TH1.0 data, 2019+: 201.2 days (published 207). TH1.0, 2024+: 131.3 days (published 143).
  - TH1.1 data (models from 2023), 2023+: 128.7 days (95% CI 106.3-158.2). TH1.1, 2024+: 102.2 days (95% CI 81.6-126.1).
  - Frontier: Claude Opus 4.6 (released 2026-02-05) p50 = 11.98 h, p80 = 1.16 h. These match the figures Wikipedia attributes to METR's time-horizons page (11 h 59 min and 1 h 10 min), which we could not open at source; METR's time-horizons page (read 29 Oct 2026, last updated 8 Sep 2026, "no longer actively updated") says measurements above 16 h are unreliable.
  - Logistic slope (median over agents released from 2025): 0.597, giving H50/H_r = 5.0 at r = 0.8 (consistent with the published 4-6x), 12.8 at 0.9, 30 at 0.95, 207 at 0.99. Above 0.8 this is an extrapolation of the logistic form.
  - Tokens: frontier agents use a median 0.259 million tokens per human-hour of task on successful runs (bin medians 0.17-0.63; elasticity of tokens to task length 0.98). METR does not document whether `tokens_count` includes cached input.
  - Cost: generation cost is recorded only for GPT-4 0314 and o1-preview; on successful runs the median is 2.4% of the human baseline cost (p90 52%); human baseline cost has a median of $143.61 per hour.
- params.yaml uses the **published** 143 days as the default T_d (range 102-240) and reports the refits alongside, as the coordinator asked.

## 4. Constructed classifications
- **licensed_signoff** (`02_occupations.py`): 1 if U.S. federal or state law requires a licensed, certificated or registered individual to perform the occupation's core regulated act or to sign for its work product. 83 codes, 11.3% of employment: CPAs (13-2011), appraisers (13-2020), investment adviser representatives (13-2052), actuaries, architects, surveyors, civil engineers, psychologists, licensed counselors and social workers, lawyers, judges and adjudicators, K-12 teachers (25-2xxx except preschool), healthcare diagnosing and treating practitioners (29-1xxx except recreational therapists and exercise physiologists), LPNs, funeral directors, insurance, securities and real estate agents, electricians, plumbers, pilots, air traffic controllers, ship officers. The basis for each is in column `licensed_basis`. This is a judgment-based classification; it is not taken from a dataset.
- **regulated_class** (`03_activities.py`, full mode): `licensed` if at least half of a DWA's employment-weighted time is in licensed_signoff occupations; otherwise `high_risk_eu` if the DWA or IWA title matches keyword rules for the EU AI Act Annex III areas (employment and worker management, creditworthiness, life and health insurance pricing, public benefits, education, law enforcement and justice, migration, biometrics, emergency triage); otherwise `none`. Rules are in the script (`ANNEX3`); the matched area is in `eu_annex3_area`. Critical infrastructure is not keyword-matched because those DWAs are physical. Annex III headings were checked by the literature agent (ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu). Result on 31.0: 1,831 none, 222 licensed, 34 high_risk_eu (employment 18, justice and law enforcement 12, education 4). Reviewing the first full build's matches removed false positives ("promotional materials" matched "promotion"; "sort ... for grading" matched "grade"; "customers or applicants" matched "applicant"), so the employment rule now needs a personnel context and the education rule a student context. The licensed class is coarse: it labels a DWA by where its time is spent, so generic DWAs done mostly by licensed staff (for example "Maintain client records") are included.
- **Time shares** (full mode): task weight = importance (IM) x mean frequency category (FT, 1-7) x relevance (RT)/100; missing ratings imputed with the occupation median (core tasks missing RT get 100); split equally over a task's DWAs; normalised within O*NET-SOC; equal-weight mean over the O*NET-SOC codes of each OEWS code. Alternatives `time_share_im_f` (no relevance) and `time_share_equal`. The spec's "importance x frequency" is the IM x F part; relevance is added so supplemental tasks few incumbents perform get less weight. O*NET does not measure time directly, so all three are proxies.
- **est_task_hours**: AEI `human_only_time_mean` (hours) from the 2026-06-26 release when downloaded (median over a DWA's tasks); otherwise assumed: task time share x 1,877 worked hours / expected annual frequency, with frequency categories mapped to 1, 4, 24, 130, 250, 750, 2,000 occurrences a year (assumed midpoints). Column `est_task_hours_method` records which.

## 5. function_occ construction
Membership and allocation shares are assumptions in `05_function_priors.py` (`FUNC_SOC`). An occupation shared by several functions has allocation shares summing to at most 1 (checked by assertion), for example software developers 0.8 product and engineering, 0.1 IT, 0.1 technical operations; customer service representatives 0.9 support, 0.1 customer success. Within a function, weight = OEWS employment x allocation, normalised to 1. National employment overweights occupations concentrated in other industries, so the industry-specific version (built automatically from OEWS May 2025 industry data) should be preferred once it exists.

## 6. Process library construction
Rules are in the docstring of `07_processes.py`: 24 processes mapped to APQC 7.4 process groups; each step written against an IWA (IDs from the O*NET 29.0 list in iwa_metrics.csv, all checked to exist) and a DWA-title keyword pattern; DWA picked, once O*NET 31.0 is present, as the matching DWA in that IWA with the largest time mass in the owning function's occupations (sum over SOC of function_occ weight x time share); critical_path = 0 for conditional, periodic or parallel steps. All volumes are assumed (`volume_assumed` = True) with ranges and a derivation note. Resolution on 31.0: all 136 steps resolved (97 by pattern plus function time mass, 27 by pattern alone, 11 by pattern plus economy-wide time mass when no function occupation performs any candidate, 1 by function mass alone). After reviewing every resolved title, 21 steps whose first pick was poor (for example "Sell agricultural products" for a SaaS renewal, "Operate recycling equipment" for production, "Schedule dining reservations" for facilities work) were pinned with anchored patterns to a DWA chosen from the IWA's 31.0 DWA list; these are judgment calls, visible in `dwa_pattern` (patterns starting with ^). P03 record-to-report and P04 month-end close overlap by design; the engine should use one or the other for a firm. Volume bases: per $M revenue, per employee, or fixed per year. P05 hires per employee should be replaced by the BLS JOLTS hires rate for the firm's industry (not retrieved in this build).

ICFR scope (29 Oct 2026, author request; rule 6 in `07_processes.py`). `processes.csv` gains `icfr_scope` (full, partial, none), `icfr_basis` and `icfr_scope_engine`; `process_activities.csv` gains `icfr_step`, `icfr_condition`, `icfr_channel`, `icfr_basis`, `icfr_source`, `typical_systems`, `financial_system_written` and `typical_systems_basis`. Process scope and the in-scope steps of partial processes start from the engine builder's `icfr_scope.csv` and `icfr_steps.csv`, copied verbatim into the script as read at 10:34 (sha256 c447a55b... and fc4a76ed..., asserted), with "class b, PCAOB AS 2201; common SOX scoping" appended to each basis. **The data contract is the single source**: since 10:45 the engine's two files mirror this output, so the script no longer reads them. `typical_systems` is a constructed prior (class d, no dataset): the system classes each step usually reads or writes. Under the author's principle (processes, systems, integrations and policies that affect the timeliness, accuracy and completeness of financial reporting), nine steps the engine lists had not flagged are in scope, each with a condition where it applies: P05 step 5 (new-hire pay data in the HRIS), P07 step 5 (refunds and credits in billing), P08 step 6 (CRM-to-billing integration), P11 step 4 (fixes that change data or configuration in an in-scope system), P13 steps 3 and 4 (service credits; renewals that amend billing), P14 step 3 (time and progress entries feeding percent-complete revenue or billing, which closes the progress-input gap), P15 step 5 (settlements feeding an ASC 450 accrual or disclosure) and P21 step 4 (scrap and yield in the ERP). This raises P07, P08, P11, P13 and P15 from none to partial. `icfr_channel` says why a step is in scope: process, system, integration or policy (several separated by "; "). Result: 8 full, 12 partial, 4 none processes; 66 of 136 steps in scope (channels: process 49, system 9, system and integration 4, policy 3, integration 1). `10_validate.py` checks that every process has a scope and a basis, every partial process has a flagged step, every step of a full process is flagged and no step of a none process is, and that the channel is set exactly for flagged steps with allowed values.

## 9. Limitations to carry into the paper
- OEWS wages are May 2025; payroll loading is June 2026 (ECEC); API prices are October 2026. The engine should index wages forward if it prices 2026-27 decisions.
- Exposure labels are on O*NET 27.2 tasks and the physical classification on O*NET 29.0; tasks new in 31.0 are unlabelled (coverage 99.7% of time mass; per-DWA coverage in `exposure_coverage`).
- AEI usage is Claude.ai conversations only (consumer and Cowork plans), April-May 2026, and is missing for DWAs below AEI's publication threshold; missing is not zero. AEI task hours are model estimates of how long the conversation's task would take a human, not time studies.
- O*NET time shares are proxies (importance x frequency x relevance), not measured time.
- METR horizons are measured on software tasks; transfer to other activities is a parameter, not an estimate.
- After the assumptions audit (Section 7f), the full-review cells of the review fraction, the outsourcing level, parallel-run lengths, U.S. severance, decision latency, the reorganization freeze, change capacity, maintenance and the capture share rest on stated precedents (class b), and inference cost per human-hour on METR's recorded costs (class c). Sampled-review cells, coordination and risk premia, duplication and reconciliation, redeployment, break fees, the J-curve, rollback, learning, bandwidth, most leadership and context parameters, family-firm presets, pricing migration, seat elasticity, incident hazards and most ancillary costs remain class d: scenario dimensions whose results must be reported as conditional. The engine's tornado should vary every class b and d parameter over its stated range.
- Product and security blocks (Sections 17-18): list prices are as of 29 Oct 2026 and change often (HubSpot halved its per-unit price in April 2026); Bass coefficients are for consumer durables, with no verified software estimate; seat-compression evidence is thin (one official occupational projection, one company statement); inference per conversation, pricing-migration costs, incident hazard, maintenance shares and most ancillary costs are assumed. Breach costs are averages over breached firms (IBM/Ponemon), not hazards; compliance costs are vendor estimates without method. `10_validate.py` does not yet list `product` or `security_and_maintenance` among the required params.yaml keys (it still checks their source and assumed flags through `walk`).
