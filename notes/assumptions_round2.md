### 1.1 Success probability: is 0.54 pilot attrition or transformation failure?

**Current.** `project_outcomes.genai_poc_to_production_rate` 0.54 [0.05, 0.70], class a (survey, flagged); P1 central varpi 0.54, fast 0.80, slow 0.30; engine `success.a0` 3.0 calibrated so a reference process succeeds about 60%.

**Evidence.**
- S&P Global Market Intelligence survey as reported by CIO Dive, 14 Mar 2025 (https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-project-fail-data-SPGlobal/742590/; secondary; read 29 Oct 2026): more than 1,000 respondents in North America and Europe; "the average organization scrapped 46% of AI proof-of-concepts before they reached production"; 42% of companies abandoned most of their AI initiatives (17% a year earlier). **What it measures:** attrition of proofs of concept before production. A proof of concept is a pilot, not a funded migration; abandonment includes cost and priority decisions taken before any attempt at cut-over. It is an upper bound on migration failure before production and says nothing about durable savings. **Match to varpi ("an attempt works", i.e. a funded transition reaches sustained operation): poor.**
- Gartner survey, Q4 2023, 644 respondents (press release 7 May 2024; the gartner.com page returned a client error; figure read via secondary reports already recorded in params.yaml): 48% of AI projects make it into production, about 8 months from prototype to production. Same concept as S&P (pilot attrition).
- BCG, "Flipping the Odds of Digital Transformation Success", 29 Oct 2020 (https://www.bcg.com/publications/2020/increasing-odds-of-success-in-digital-transformation; read 29 Oct 2026): 825 senior executives, self-rated; 30% "met or exceeded their target value and resulted in sustainable change" (win zone); 44% created some value but missed targets with limited long-term change (worry zone); 26% created under 50% of target value with no sustainable change (woe zone); with all six success factors, 80%. **What it measures:** whole-transformation outcomes, self-reported. The win-plus-worry share (74%) is the share of transformations that created value; the woe share (26%) is the closest analog of an attempt that fails.
- McKinsey Global Survey, "Losing from day one: Why even successful transformations fall short", 7 Dec 2021 (https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/successful-transformations; read 29 Oct 2026): 1,034 participants who had been part of a transformation in the past five years, fielded 18 May to 29 Jun 2021; "less than one-third" reported improved and sustained performance; successful transformations realized "only 67 percent of the maximum financial benefits"; all others "an average of only 37 percent"; about half of the value is realized in the first 18 months; in a separate database of 82 listed companies, top-quartile performers "capture 74 percent of their transformations' value within the first 12 months". **What it measures:** the share of targeted value realized, unconditionally and by outcome, at the program level. Self-reported consultancy survey (flag), but the only source that measures realization rather than attrition. The unconditional mean, 0.30 x 0.67 + 0.70 x 0.37 = 0.46, is the concept the model's per-attempt expected realization (varpi x b_bar x disruption haircut) represents.

**Finding.** The 0.54 is pilot attrition, not transformation failure, as IB-5 and EX-4 say. Used as "an attempt works" it is too low for funded migrations (it counts pilots dropped on cost or priority) and, combined with b_bar = 0.85 and the disruption haircut exp(-0.23 x 2) = 0.63, gives a per-attempt expected realization of 0.29, below every outcome survey (McKinsey mean 0.46; even the McKinsey "all other transformations" mean of 0.37). The disruption haircut double counts: survey outcomes already include leadership changes and strategy shifts.

**Proposal.** varpi = 0.70 [0.54, 0.80], class b: central = BCG's share of transformations that created value (74%) rounded down for the self-report bias; low end = pilot attrition (S&P 0.54); high end = BCG's six-factor rate (0.80). b_bar = 0.67 [0.37, 0.85], class a (flagged): McKinsey's realized share for successful transformations; low end the "all others" mean; high end the top quartile. Drop the exp(-nu_D T_emb) factor from q when varpi and b_bar come from these surveys; keep nu_D in the shock results (prop:ext-shocks), which price the timing of disruptions, not the base rate. Product varpi x b_bar = 0.47 [0.20, 0.68], consistent with McKinsey's unconditional 0.46. Engine: recalibrate `success.a0` so a reference process reaches production with probability about 0.50 (Gartner 48%, S&P 54%) only if the engine's b_bar carries the realization haircut; otherwise calibrate the product to 0.46.

**Files.** P1: sections/horizons.tex (Pace, Risk and Embedding bullets; "Under slow execution" sentence), sections/model.tex (ass:program, def:attainable(b), prop:attainable, prop:expected), sections/appendix_proofs.tex, sections/abstract.tex and sections/intro.tex (execution sentences), paper1/macros.tex (\PaceSuccCen, \PaceSuccFast, \PaceQCen, \PaceRateCen, \PaceCenEight/One/Six, \PaceCrossCen), code/calibrate_horizons.py (PACE dict), code/paper1_macros.py. ### 1.2 Change capacity kappa_abs

**Current.** `change_capacity.affected_fte_share_per_year` 0.10 [0.05, 0.20], class b grounded on JOLTS layoffs (1.0% a month) and single restructurings (6 to 13%).

**Evidence.**
- World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025, digest (https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/digest/; read 29 Oct 2026): over 1,000 employers representing more than 14 million workers in 55 economies; "if the world's workforce was made up of 100 people, 59 would need training by 2030" (29 upskilled in role, 19 upskilled and redeployed, 11 unlikely to receive it); structural churn of 22% of today's jobs over 2025 to 2030; 40% of employers plan to reduce staff where skills become less relevant. **What it measures:** employers' expected share of workers whose work changes (training, redeployment), about 12 a year. This is the concept kappa_abs needs (employees whose work changes), where layoffs measure exits.
- Salesforce Form 8-K, 4 Jan 2023 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1108524/000110852423000003/crm-20230104.htm; read 29 Oct 2026): reduction of "approximately 10 percent" of the workforce; charges $1.4 to 2.1 billion; employee actions "substantially complete by the end of the Company's fiscal 2024" (31 Jan 2024); real estate actions complete in fiscal 2026.
- Meta Q4 and full-year 2023 results (https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2024/Meta-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2023-Results-Initiates-Quarterly-Dividend/default.aspx; read 29 Oct 2026): headcount 67,317 at 31 Dec 2023, down 22% year over year; restructuring charges $3.45 billion in 2023; operating margin 35% (2023) against 25% (2022); Q4 41% against 20%.
- Alphabet (already in params.yaml `transition.severance_s_w_months`): about 12,000 roles, 6%, Jan 2023.
- BLS JOLTS (already in params.yaml): layoffs and discharges 1.0% a month, total separations 3.2% (Aug 2026).

**Finding.** The panel is right that layoffs measure exits, not work changed. The WEF figure (12% a year) measures the right concept and lands on the same value. Realized single-year reductions at public software and internet firms in 2023 ran from 6% to 22% of headcount, so 20% is not an extreme for a cost program.

**Proposal.** Keep 0.10 [0.05, 0.22], class b; replace the grounding text: WEF 2025 (work changed, 12 a year) as the central precedent; filings (6 to 22% in one year) and JOLTS as the exit-based bracket. The upper bound moves from 0.20 to 0.22 (Meta 2023).

Central 0.10 = geometric middle of the field analogs.
- Price path: kappa_fixed(H) = kappa_2025 / D^(H - 2025) with D = 5 [3, 10] a year (Gundlach, Epoch; class a), floored at prices.floor_share (0.005, d). kappa_front = 0.33 [0.10, 0.50], class d bounded by the value test kappa < 1 - review share: the marginal task is automated only while compute costs less than the labor it replaces, so the frontier cost rise (3 to 18x a year) is capped by abandonment of the task, not extrapolated.
- Mix: omega_H, the share of automated labor cost in tasks reached within two years of H (frontier-priced): 0.6 [0.3, 0.9] in 2028, 0.4 [0.2, 0.7] in 2031, 0.25 [0.1, 0.5] in 2036 (class d; falls as the feasible set grows ahead of the program).
- c_c(H) = s_L x [omega_H kappa_front + (1 - omega_H) kappa_fixed(H)].

**Result.** c_c at 2025 prices (no path): 0.06 [0.01, 0.20], b-anchored. By horizon: 2028 0.12 [0.02, 0.30]; 2031 0.08 [0.01, 0.25]; 2036 0.05 [0.01, 0.20]; class d (the mix and the frontier cap are scenarios), with the 2025 anchor class b. The paper's 0.20 sits at the high end of the 2028 range and above the 2031 and 2036 centrals; the 0.40 high-compute case exceeds the value-test cap and should be dropped or labelled infeasible at full automation.

## 6. Common-mode error correlation across AI providers

**Current.** P1 Ass. 7 and Lemma 4(e) treat failures of different providers as independent with a Herfindahl term H_cm; params_reliability `common_mode.provider_correlation` 1.0 [0.3, 1.0] within a provider, across providers d; engine reliability.provider_correlation.

**Evidence.** Kim, Garg, Peng and Garg, "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models", ICML 2025, PMLR 267 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962v1 and /html/2506.07962v1 and the PDF; read 29 Oct 2026). Data: 349 models on the HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard v2 (12,032 MMLU multiple-choice questions, mostly ten options), 71 models from 15 companies on Stanford HELM (14,042 MMLU questions, four options), and 20 models on the authors' resume-screening task (1,800 resume-job pairs, 450 hand-labeled). Measure: for a pair of models, the share of questions on which both are wrong *and give the same wrong answer*; the independence baseline is 1/3 on HELM and about 0.127 on HuggingFace. Results: mean error agreement 0.60 on HELM and 0.423 on HuggingFace, about twice the baseline; 97.5% (HELM) and 100% (HuggingFace) of pairs above baseline; same company (+0.066 HF, +0.022 HELM), same architecture (+0.076 HF) and similar size raise agreement; more accurate models have more correlated errors, strongest when both are accurate; R^2 0.34 (HF), 0.61 (HELM), 0.42 (resumes, residual correlation with human ratings); extreme pairs 0.97 (two Llama models) and 0.9987 (text-unicorn and palmyra-x-v3). Agreement "when either model is wrong" is lower (regression intercepts about 0.24). The paper does not report P(model B wrong | model A wrong) or the ratio of joint to product error rates. Implications: an LLM judge inflates the accuracy of models that share its errors; a single hiring model excludes about 20% of applicants systemically.

**Finding.** Verified, with the concept corrected: "agree on 60% of their errors" means that when two models both err on an MMLU item, they give the identical wrong answer 60% of the time. It is not the probability that a second provider fails given the first fails, which is what a two-layer control design needs; it does show that (i) joint errors are far above independence and rise with model accuracy, so frontier models are the most correlated, and (ii) when both layers err, disagreement-based checks would not catch it 60% of the time. The domain is academic multiple-choice questions and resume ratings, not enterprise transactions.

**Proposal.** Add to params_reliability `common_mode`: `cross_provider_identical_error_share_given_joint_error` 0.60 [0.42, 0.60], class a for the benchmark, b for transfer to enterprise tasks; keep the cross-provider joint-error correlation d (unmeasured) and, as the RA panel says, state that H_cm is a lower bound on effective concentration because providers share base models, cloud regions and corpora. In P1 Ass. 7 and P2 prop:ext-commonmode and prop:acct-amin, cite the paper for the direction and the 60% for the disagreement-check failure rate, not as p_cm.

**Files.** P1: sections/model.tex (Ass. 7, lem:fullcost(e)), sections/horizons.tex (common-mode cost bullet), sections/discussion.tex, sections/literature.tex, sections/appendix_proofs.tex (a_min example common-mode probabilities 0.002 and 0.02), sections/appendix_protocol.tex, code/calibrate_horizons.py (c_err_cm). P2: part2/theory_ext.tex (prop:ext-commonmode), part2/theory_accounting.tex (prop:acct-amin, the independence paragraph the RA panel drafted), part2/theory_core.tex, part2/theory_labor.tex, proofs_ext.tex, proofs_accounting.tex, part2/refs_reliability.bib (add kim2025correlated), code/verify_part2_ext.py, code/verify_part2_accounting.py. P3: data/tasks/params_reliability.yaml (common_mode block), engine defaults.yaml (reliability.provider_correlation, cm_burst), risk.py, security.py, formulas.py, report.py.

**Effect.** Raises the common-mode term c_err_cm (today 0.33% of revenue in the central ceiling) when the all-AI control layers are on different providers; lowers the value of provider diversity in prop:acct-amin(a) and Prop. 10(a).
