Not a forecast. Every input moves together over the range the paper itself states. "Holds everywhere" means no sampled point, box corner or targeted search broke the claim. Shares of draws assume every input is equally likely anywhere in its range, which no data measures.

Words used on this page

Rule of 40 score
A company's revenue growth rate plus its profit margin, both in percent. Investors use 40 as a pass mark for software companies.
Rule of 100
A score of 100: the level some people expect AI automation to make possible. The paper argues it is not a lasting state for a mature company.
Draw (one "world")
One run of the paper's model with one particular set of assumptions. We ran thousands, each with a different mix.
Stated range
For each assumption, the lowest and highest values the paper itself says are reasonable. Every draw stays inside these ranges.
Central value
The answer the paper gives with all assumptions at their middle settings.
Central basis
The paper's main way of treating sales and marketing: only spending above a maintenance level counts as buying growth. The two other bases count all of it or none of it.
G* (growth threshold)
How fast a company's market must grow each year before full AI automation can lift its score to 100.
Payback
The number of years until the savings from automation repay the cost and disruption of making the change.

Cost floors: which ones matter, and what controls cost

Cost floors are the costs that automation cannot remove: taxes, payment fees, audits and SOX controls, security, maintenance and the cost of AI errors. The first chart shows how much each one moves the growth threshold. The other two test the accountability floor, the cost of controls a public company needs to keep its financial reporting reliable, on its own set of assumptions.
