### 1.1 prop:expected: program clock, random freezes, fluid version (R1-1, FT-6, FT-7)
- Def. `def:attainable`(b), model.tex. Old: "Reorganizations arrive at rate nu^R and freeze the program for T_frz
  each, which slows its clock by the factor 1 + nu^R T_frz". New: an explicit assumption that freezes slow the
  program's clock uniformly (program time advances at rate 1/(1 + nu^R T_frz)); waves and embedding lags run in
  program time; the disruption hazards nu_D run in calendar time (the turnover and strategy data are per calendar
  year), so a flight lasts (1 + nu^R T_frz) T_emb in calendar time and p^emb = varpi exp{-nu_D (1 + nu^R T_frz) T_emb}.
- Paragraph after the definition: grounds each of the three assumptions (uniform clock as a mean-field reading,
  justified by part (f); calendar-time hazards; fixed realized share per process, grounded in how
  `mckinsey2021losing` measures realized value), and states that correlated disruptions leave (c) and (e)(i)-(ii)
  unchanged but raise variance (R1-13(d)).
- Prop. `prop:expected`(f), new: Poisson freezes in program time, each lasting T_frz of calendar time. Program time
  over calendar time tends to 1/(1 + nu^R T_frz) almost surely; the bounds become E R^att(t~(t)) and
  varpi e^{-nu_D T_emb} n_cap E n(t~(t)); single-attempt survival exp{-nu_D T_emb - nu^R T_emb (1 - e^{-nu_D T_frz})},
  at least the uniform-clock value; the uniform-clock bounds can fail at a finite date (R1's counterexample is
  reproduced: P = 0.607, check 28o). Proof in appendix_proofs.tex, proof of prop:expected (f).
- Prop. `prop:expected`(e), new: divisible program (continuum of identical processes, wave capacity
  c_w = min_i Gamma_i / C^i as a share of the program). (i) E(t) <= min{1, p^emb c_w n(t_R)} under every plan and
  E R* = R*(theta_0) + b-bar E(t) Delta R-hat; (ii) the greedy plan attains equality while
  c_w[1 + varpi(n(t_R) - 1)] <= 1; (iii) with nu_D = 0 it falls short of the bound by less than c_w and E -> 1. This
  is the object `code/calibrate_horizons.py::pace` computes (c_w = rho T_par, q = p^emb); check 28m confirms the
  code's formula equals the greedy value under central execution through 2036 and is within one wave's capacity
  under fast execution, and that the lumpy model with n_cap = floor(c_w N) converges to the fluid one.
- Intuition after the proposition: "risks act like a cut in capacity" became "for programs with many waves these
  risks act approximately like a cut in the program's pace by the factor p^emb/(1 + nu^R T_frz)", and freezes
  "dilate time, the embedding lag included".
- Prop. `prop:expected`(b): monotonicity in nu^R T_frz now holds under the uniform-clock assumption; under random
  freezes the law depends on nu^R and T_frz separately (stated in (f) and its intuition).
- horizons.tex, "Pace": now cites prop:expected(e), writes m_0(t) = m_0A + b-bar E(t)(m_0F - m_0A), says the
  calibration uses the upper bound and how far it can overstate. "Embedding": the outcome-survey success rates
  already include disruptions, so the calibration reads p^emb directly (matching the code's q = varpi after the
  29 October parameter commit).

### 1.5 RA-1: accountability floor as a pass/fail ICFR constraint
Formulation as written (model.tex, Proposition `prop:amin`(b) and (c)):

> (b) (Admissibility.) A design is admissible if and only if no in-scope control objective is left with a reasonable
> possibility that a material misstatement is not prevented or detected on a timely basis. This is the
> material-weakness standard of AS 2201 (Appendix A, paragraph A7) and Item 308(a)(3) of Regulation S-K, under which
> a single material weakness bars a conclusion that internal control over financial reporting is effective. With a
> reasonable possibility modeled as a probability above pi-bar in (0,1) in the period, D is admissible if and only if
> pi_o(D) <= pi-bar, equivalently P_o(D) <= P-bar_o := 1 - (1 - pi-bar)^{1/N_o}, for every o in O. If
> p^cc(theta) + (1 - p^cc(theta)) prod_{j in J} p^cm_j(theta) >= P-bar_o for every theta in Theta, no design for
> objective o that uses only types in the set J is admissible, however many and however cheap its layers; in
> particular, an all-AI design on a single provider j can be admissible only if p^cc(theta) + (1 - p^cc(theta))
> p^cm_j(theta) < P-bar_o for some theta; while its common-mode bound is at or above P-bar_o, only better IT general
> controls or a layer of another type help, and more spending on AI layers does not.
>
> (c) (The floor.) The accountability floor is the minimum cost over admissible designs,
> a_min = (1/S) min_{D admissible} [C^acct + sum_a C^itgc_a(theta) + sum_o sum_j n_oj c_j
> + sum_o N^nm_o L^nm_o P_o(D)], with a_min = infinity if no design is admissible. The expected loss from material
> misstatements is not traded against cost inside the constraint: an inadmissible design does not lower the floor
> however cheap it is. Only errors below materiality enter as an expected cost.

- Assumption `ass:layers` rewritten: control objectives O with N_o opportunities, layers n_oj of type j, ITGC quality
  theta with cost C^itgc_a(theta) per in-scope AI application a, a cross-type common cause p^cc(theta) (management
  override, defective shared data or IPE, ITGC failure, reviewers' acceptance of wrong AI output), type common modes
  p^cm_j(theta), both nonincreasing in theta, and errors below materiality (N^nm_o, L^nm_o).
- Prop. `prop:amin`(a) P_o(D) formula with p^cc; (d) scale: admissible set independent of S, floor nonincreasing in
  S (FT-25), limit set by errors below materiality.
- Intuition quotes Item 308(a)(3) and AS 2201 A7 and its FAS 5 footnote (verified, Section 6), says the standard sets
  no number for "remote", so pi-bar is a judgment.
- Remark `rem:amin-values` recomputed as an admissibility example (pi-bar = 5% and 1%): no all-AI design passes
  (at least 1 - 0.995^24 = 11.3% a year); all-human designs pass only at 5% with three layers ($1.50 million); the
  cheapest admissible design is one human over one AI layer at 5% (3.1% a year, $0.90 million) and one human over two
  AI layers at 1% (0.7%, $0.95 million); 48 opportunities or an AI common mode of 0.01 need two human layers over one
  AI layer ($1.40 million). The remark says the human common mode of 0.002 is high for an effective control
  environment (the parameters illustrate the mechanism). Checks 25d, 25e, 27a-27c.
- horizons.tex "Accountability floor" restated: pass-fail standard, cheapest admissible design, all-AI single-provider
