What no available data can answer yet

  • Whether your growth spending earns back its cost. What would answer it: payback and retention by customer cohort at a stated discount rate.
  • Why the top of the score distribution fell. The data cannot separate fewer fast growers from lower margins among them.
  • What AI will be able to do, and when. The paper assumes every process can be automated from the start. Measured task length has doubled about every —, but that is outside the model.
  • Private AI-native companies and companies outside US public software. They are not in SEC filings; answering needs private-company or non-US filings data.
  • Whether a higher score predicts share-price returns. SEC filings carry one market value a year, not a price series; answering needs a price database.

Words used on this page

Rule of 40 score
Revenue growth plus profit margin, both in percent. Here the margin is annual free cash flow after stock-based compensation and capital expenditure.
Durable score
The part of the score that lasts once the market is full: the margin a company earns at market growth, plus market growth net of capital needs.
Position
A typical kind of company, such as the growth corner or the mature cash generator, taken as the medians of real firm-years.
Lever
A cost line AI can shrink: engineering, the back office, the growth engine or cost of revenue.
Back office (G&A)
General and administrative cost: finance, HR, legal and administration.
Growth engine
The part of sales and marketing that buys growth. A better engine buys the same growth for less.
Maintenance level
The sales and marketing a company spends just to keep its customers; on the central basis only spend above it buys growth.
Pass-through
The share of a cost saving that competition hands to customers as lower prices.
Change capacity
How much of its work a company can change in a year. It sets the pace of the program.
Changes in flight
Transitions under way and not yet paying off. Their cost is the dip in the score during the program.
Payback
Years until the program's cumulative gains repay its cumulative transition costs. It comes later than the year when yearly gains first exceed yearly costs.
Rule-of-100 threshold
How fast a market must grow each year for a fully automated company to reach a cash score of —.
Compute share
The cost of AI computing as a share of revenue once work is automated.
Full-cost floors
The recurring costs of maintaining, securing and supervising AI, including the losses from its errors.
Accountability floor
The cost of audits, internal controls and the accountable people behind them, which automation does not remove.
Simulated world
One run of the paper's model with one mix of assumptions, each inside the range the paper states. The stress test ran a million per seed.
Public float
The market value of shares held by people outside the company's insiders, as each company states it in its annual report.
Multiple
Market value divided by revenue.
Central value
The paper's answer with every assumption at its middle setting.
Sobol index
The share of the differences between worlds that one group of assumptions explains, counting how it interacts with the others.
