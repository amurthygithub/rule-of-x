**M3. Pass-through: the main text does not say whose unit-cost saving sets the price cut, and the same symbol denotes the rate and the elasticity.**
- Location: sections/horizons.tex line 50 (PDF p. 56), "We let the industry pass the share $\varphi$ of every unit-cost saving to its price"; sections/tables/tab_passthrough_recon.tex (Table S2, supplement p. 5); sections/supp_theory.tex line 142 (Lemma S2, $\varphi$ := elasticity); sections/model.tex line 199 (Proposition AI (iv), $\varphi$ = elasticity); Proposition 9 (a) and (d) ($(1-\varphi)c_h$); Table 6 row label and Table S9 ($\varphi$ = rate).
- Evidence (re-derivation, independent of the code): with the central cost structure ($\tau$ 0.18, $f$ 0.025, $c_c$ 0.06, $a_{\min}$ 0.03, $c_{\rm full}$ 0.0623, base 0.6746, $G^*$ 32.5%), the full-cost non-fee unit cost is $K = 0.1523$ per unit of today's price and the anchor's implied cost is $C_0 = 1 - f - m_{0A}/(1-\tau) = 0.6019$ ($m_{0A}$ 0.3060, netted median). Lemma S2(a) gives $d\gamma/du = -(1/p)(1 - u p'/p)$; for a pass-through rate $\varrho = dp/du$ the elasticity is $\varrho u/p$ (0.30 at $\varrho$ = 0.5, 0.42 at 0.7, as Table S2 prints). Price $1 - \varrho S$ with $S = C_0 - K = 0.4496$, fees proportional to price, other costs fixed per unit: margin $(1-\tau)(1 - f - K/(1-\varrho S))$ = 0.6384 and 0.6173, so $G^*$ = 36.16 and 38.27%: the printed 36.2 and 38.3. Every other row of Table S2 also reproduces (referee's reading 33.41/33.80; cost-of-revenue only 33.73/34.28; all-S&M anchor 34.83/36.01; no-S&M anchor 37.02/39.87; first version 37.92/40.08). Which reading is right: the referee's number rests on (i) exposing only the cost-of-revenue saving, which the referee himself said contradicts Proposition 4(d), and (ii) the factor $1-\varphi(1-H)$, which is the share of a dollar saving a Cournot firm keeps once its volume gain is counted and does not belong in a margin share when costs are per unit. The central reading is therefore the one consistent with the paper's own model, and the referee's "about 32%" (33.4 to 33.8 on the new base) is a different scenario, not a correction; the paper reports both, which is right. Two things the text does not say: the saving $S$ is the top-tail anchor firm's own saving, from its implied cost of 60% of price, which is smaller than an industry-typical firm's; with the typical $1-5bn firm's netted intercept of S6 (18.5 points) the same arithmetic gives 37.9 and 41.7%, and with a 10-point intercept 39.3 and 44.6%. This dependence is disclosed only in Table S2's notes ("$S$ the firm's whole unit-cost saving from today's cost, $1-f-m_{0A}/(1-\tau)$"), and Table S2 has no typical-firm row. And $\varphi$ means the elasticity in Lemma S2, Proposition AI (iv) and Proposition 9, and the rate in Section 7.2, Table 6, Table S2 and S2.8; the IO referee asked for distinct symbols and the response (IO2-N1 "Fixed") does not mention it. Lemma S2's $u$ is the cost of revenue, while the scenario exposes overhead too; within the model, where every cost is a share of revenue, that extension holds, but a clause saying so would close the gap. (The check 25g of verify_theory.py confirms the first-order slope; it does not test the symbol or the baseline.)
- Fix: in Section 7.2 say that the price cut is the top-tail firm's own unit-cost saving and that an industry-typical saving would raise the thresholds by about 2 to 4 points; add a typical-firm row to Table S2; give the rate its own symbol (for example $\varrho$) in Section 7.2, Table 6, Table S2, S2.8 and Table S9; one clause after Lemma S2(a) on costs beyond the cost of revenue.
