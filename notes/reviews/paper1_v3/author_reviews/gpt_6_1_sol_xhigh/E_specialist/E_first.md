## Findings requiring revision

### E1. The execution rate conflates value creation with durable embedding and joins incompatible outcome strata

**Locators:** main §7.3 pp. 59–61, Table 5 p. 60; supplement S2.9 p. 9 and Table S11 p. 36. **Classification:** major source transport / calibration; not a misquotation.

The primary [BCG report](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2020/increasing-odds-of-success-in-digital-transformation) confirms 30% win, 44% worry, and 26% woe outcomes from executive assessments. The worry category has limited lasting change; even woe can have limited value. Thus 74% is neither a measured probability of durable embedding nor a clean positive-value versus zero-value split. The [McKinsey survey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organization/our-insights/successful-transformations) confirms 67% of potential financial benefits for successful transformations and 37% for others. Applying 67% to both BCG win and worry groups is a cross-survey conditioning assumption. The paper's own compatible-strata alternative yields 31.5% realization by 2036 rather than 40.7%, yet the latter drives the abstract.

**Strongest author defense:** These are explicitly analogies, not forecasts; S2.9 already presents the compatible-strata and win-only alternatives, and removes a disruption haircut to avoid double counting. This substantially limits the objection. It does not make `p_emb = 0.70` an observed embedding rate, or establish the central case's privileged evidentiary status.

**Smallest correction:** label both `p_emb` and realized yield as joint scenario choices, avoid interpreting worry outcomes as permanent embedding, and place the compatible-strata case beside the central case in Table 5 and the summary. Do not infer that survey outcomes already include each modeled leadership or strategy hazard without a measured event history.

**Acceptance test:** a single event/outcome dictionary must map source success, persistence, realized benefit, and time-to-realization into model attempt, embedding, and yield. Rerun the path using category-consistent yields and show sensitivity jointly with embedding delay/capacity; clearly distinguish any case that is merely assumed. A source-identified payback claim would additionally require transition cost data, which the paper explicitly lacks.

### E2. Training need is not annual change capacity

**Locators:** main p. 59, capacity bullet; supplement S2.9 p. 9 and S11 p. 36. **Classification:** major source transport, locally repairable wording/classification.

The [WEF digest](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025) reports the share of workers expected to need training by 2030, not completed process migrations or a throughput ceiling. Need over five years cannot establish how much work an organization can change each year: people may be trained repeatedly, training can accompany augmentation, and expected unmet training is included. The paper's description that this survey measures how much workers' work changes annually is incorrect. Layoffs and economy-wide exits also do not identify this missing conversion.

**Strongest author defense:** capacity is class b, and the one-for-one map from affected employees to program benefit is explicitly assumed. A 10% annual scenario is permissible.

**Smallest correction:** call 10% and its range scenario values loosely contextualized by training and restructuring evidence; remove the claim that WEF measures the needed concept. **Acceptance test:** define the numerator (employee work materially changed, process cost, or savings-weighted program share), denominator, clock, and repeat changes. Either document a cohort-based throughput estimate or present the path as a scenario surface in this unidentified parameter. This matters because execution is the largest 2036 sensitivity.

### E3. The full-cost error rate has no common unit or demonstrated transport to a firm's automated work

**Locators:** main pp. 56–57; supplement S11 p. 39, Lemma S4(c) p. 49, Table S16 p. 52. **Classification:** major measurement / dimensional transport; class-a classification unsupported as used.

The final [Asgari article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01670-7) confirms 191 hallucinated sentences out of 12,999 generated sentences, and 1,712 omissions out of 49,590 transcript sentences. These are different denominators. The 450 pairs arise from 18 configurations of 25 transcripts, not 450 independent encounters, using direct-prompted GPT-4. Adding the two percentages does not estimate errors per one common output unit. The [Magesh author preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20362) instead measures faulty legal answers per query. Neither identifies a cost-weighted firm-wide per-unit rate of 0.050 or 0.085. Table S16's characterization as retrieval-grounded work is also not true of the clinical study.

**Strongest author defense:** loss severities and much of the floor are explicit scenarios; the paper stresses transfer limits and reports a wide cost range. It also correctly declines to use human catch rates as AI catch rates. The defect is the measurement status of the transported rate, not a claim that operational errors cost nothing.

**Smallest correction:** reclassify the used rates as class d, retain the source studies only as task-specific precedents, and define the work unit in Lemma S4(c). Avoid a universal lower floor inferred from these averages. **Acceptance test:** provide a dimensional accounting of error counts, output/work cost weights, overlap, severity, and review state. Recompute a task-stratified floor or report a sensitivity surface for an explicitly unidentified loss share. Mark the 32.5% threshold conditional on that loss share as well as compute.

### E4. METR and AI product margins do not supply credible bounds on enterprise automation compute

**Locators:** main pp. 55–58 and 69; supplement S2.8 p. 9, S11 p. 37. **Classification:** major source transport / uncertainty language; quotation substantially correct.

The [Kwa paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14499v4) supports its quoted successful-run cost comparison at an assumed high-skilled wage. That is conditional generation cost on a software/ML task suite, excluding enterprise integration, failed attempts, and acceptance work. The author's derived 0.4–1.1% statistic was not independently reproduced: the public file was located, but its contents were not returned by available access paths. [ICONIQ's official report page](https://www.iconiq.com/growth/reports/state-of-ai-2026) confirms the product gross-margin and internal-spend numbers. Product cost is normalized by AI-product revenue, while internal automation cost is normalized by firm revenue; neither transport provides an empirical upper bound on the other.

**Strongest author defense:** the 6% central compute share is expressly a scenario; the paper correctly states the measurement gap and distinguishes sold products from own-work automation. These concessions should be retained.

**Smallest correction:** replace 'credible bounds' with a declared scenario domain, and treat the 60% endpoint as a maintained same-output value-test assumption. **Acceptance test:** pin the METR run-data revision and publish an executable aggregate with all-run costs per accepted task, model/task weighting, human baseline and success treatment. Build a bridge from task cost to revenue share. For enterprise claims, show recurrent compute, implementation, review, and ongoing maintenance separately and identify double counting. No new enterprise estimate is required if the headline remains purely conditional.

### E5. A close opportunity-frontier antecedent should be compared explicitly

**Locators:** main §§2.1, 2.2, 2.7 and opening §3, pp. 7–12. **Classification:** moderate novelty positioning; no demonstrated theorem duplication.

[Wood (2025), §2.1/Figure 1](https://academic.oup.com/cje/article/49/5/1073/8315769), revisiting Wood (1975), uses a downward frontier between profit margin on sales and sales-revenue growth. Its §3.1 equations (5)–(6) choose a shareholder optimum on that frontier using `V = R₀π/(i−g)`. Differentiating gives `π′(g) + V/R₀ = 0` at an interior optimum, directly connecting the supporting growth weight to the revenue multiple in steady state. This is closer to the paper's geometry than a generic citation to dividend discounting. The original book's Google Books preview also flags heterogeneous frontier locations as the reason cross-firm patterns need not reveal positions on one common frontier; the original full passage was not inspected.

**Strongest author defense:** the paper already credits Marris, Solow, customer capital and marginal q, expressly says its technology and first-order condition are standard, and claims a dynamic extension plus screening/decision-weight distinction. Wood does not establish duplication of the dynamic theorem, headroom budget, or empirical estimands.

**Smallest correction and acceptance test:** add the direct comparison, state precisely what dynamic, identification, or normalization result goes beyond this antecedent, and avoid presenting the revenue-multiple tangency itself as new. Check the original primary works for a final priority claim. This is not evidence of plagiarism or an intentionally omitted citation.

## Source checks that should not become allegations

- **Kim et al. (2025):** the 60% HELM and 42.3% Hugging Face figures and their conditioning are accurately stated. The paper correctly distinguishes agreement given both models wrong from failure given another model wrong. These results motivate dependence, but do not measure the illustrative common-mode probabilities in Proposition 5. See [primary article and linked paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html).
- **Gundlach et al.:** the fixed-performance price decline and rising record-performance run-cost directions are supported; the software benchmark's uncertainty is explicitly reported by the paper. Extrapolation to the compute-share path remains a scenario. See [version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23455v2).
- **Deeter and Bondy:** the 2–3 public-company weighting, historical 2–9 regression range and reported R-squared comparison are verified. They are practitioner screening relationships, as the manuscript says, not identified causal decision weights. See [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-rule-of-x).
- **Vitorino (2014):** the author's full-text copy supports shadow values of brand/physical capital and endogenous advertising/value relationships. It corroborates the manuscript's acknowledgment of marginal-q ancestry; it does not estimate this paper's growth elasticity. See [author copy](https://www.maria-ana-vitorino.com/docs/Vitorino2014.pdf).
