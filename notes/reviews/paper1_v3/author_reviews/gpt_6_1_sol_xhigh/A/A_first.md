## Assessment

The central static frontier, support-line solution, score budget, and conditional dynamic verification result survive this audit. The paper accurately discloses that its differentiable HJB solution is a verification hypothesis, not an existence theorem. Treating this admission as a disproved theorem would be unfair. Independently solved dynamic programming reproduces the worked weights at the reported shares. The main theoretical contribution is a score-specific organization of standard concave spending/optimal advertising ideas, a useful finite-share accounting budget, and explicit separation of observed choices from individual opportunities. It is not a generally identified empirical technology or universal optimal growth weight.

No major refutation of the central mathematics is established by this pass. Two narrow corrections survive a defense check: the supplement's small-share local-asymptotic discussion omits a nonautonomous forcing term, and the numerical example's decision weight is not globally decreasing with share. Neither invalidates Proposition 2's stated limits or conditional verification theorem. A finite-change wording concern is narrowed by the paper's explicit first-order qualification.

## Strongest reconstruction and dependencies

At a fixed share, growth is `g=G+ell*sigma^alpha`, `0<alpha<1`; cash margin is `m=m0-sigma-k*g`, `k>=0`. Inverting growth spend gives `m*(g)=m0-k*g-ell^(-1/alpha)*(g-G)^(1/alpha)`. It is strictly decreasing and concave, so maximizing a linear score gives a unique support point. The surplus is `Pi=max_sigma[(X-k)ell*sigma^alpha-sigma]`, with the stated closed forms. Headroom `ell=lambda(1-s/sbar)` lowers this surplus as share rises. Independently of the power functional form, `RX-Rinfty <= (X-k)(g-G)` and `d log s=(g-G)dt`; hence finite share headroom bounds the time integral of excess score. A dynamic firm instead uses the marginal value `v'(s)` as the instantaneous growth weight if a bounded C1 HJB solution verifies. An investor screen prices a different persistence experiment; its weight is a duration-scaled margin. Heterogeneous chosen observations do not identify a firm's own support slope. Free-entry competition eliminates the markup over full cost under the explicitly restricted Cournot/one-date-entry assumptions; normal capital/customer-capital returns and scarce-input rents can remain.

Material dependencies: the share-budget conclusion needs no share loss at zero spend and a single fixed market cap; the dynamic result needs H; numerical value-to-weight claims need the chosen technology/discount parameters; general industry/practical transport requires more than mathematical algebra.

## A1. Local HJB asymptotics omit an explicit forcing term

Location: supplementary printed/PDF p16, final paragraph before Lemma5 proof ('HJB equation as an ODE'). Classification: demonstrated local asymptotic error in supplementary discussion; minor to moderate technical correction, not a central theorem refutation.

The paragraph says every solution near `(0,v0)` behaves `u-v0 proportional s^((r-G)/Delta0-1)`. Let `z=s/sbar`, `rho=r-G`, `b=m0-kG`, `u=v/s`, and `a=rho/Delta0-1>0`. The displayed ODE is correct, but its linearization is

`z d(u-v0)/dz = a(u-v0)+(v0-k)z+o(|u-v0|+z)`.

The last term follows from `ell=lambda(1-z)`, whose derivative is nonzero. For a!=1 the general leading expansion includes `C*z^a+(v0-k)*z/(1-a)`; at a=1 there is a resonant `z log z` term. For a>1 the forced linear term dominates the claimed power. Full-assumption example: alpha=.5, lambda=.2, r=.1, G=0, m0=.1, k=0, sbar=1. The smallest simple positive root is v0=1.12701665379258; Delta0=.02254033307585; a=3.43649167310371; forced coefficient =-.46255715389207. Independent integration of the displayed ODE backward from z=.001 yields `(u-v0)/z=-.46255646` at z=1e-7, while `(u-v0)/z^a=-5.26e16`. This numerical illustration supports, but is not the proof of, the analytic omitted term. Parameters meet the theorem's full alpha domain, although not the narrower scenario range used in most tables.

Strongest defense: the author may have intended differences between two local solutions, which do have the homogeneous exponent, or only the homogeneous mode used to explain why the small-share boundary does not select a solution. That conclusion is valid. The sentence explicitly concerns u-v0, however, and its claimed universal leading behavior omits the forced solution. Smallest correction: state the full nonautonomous expansion or discuss differences between solutions, with resonance and exponent cases. Acceptance test: substitute proposed expansion into the ODE at a<1,a=1,a>1; show remainder order. No downstream numerical headline must change; existence remains conditional.

## A2. Worked dynamic weight falls at the reported shares but rises again near saturation

Location: main pp20-22 Cor3 intuition/worked example; supplement p6 S2.2 says 'it falls with share'. Classification: demonstrated overly broad numerical interpretation, limited to worked example and stage-ranking language.

Independent upwind HJB with 1k,2k,4k,8k,16k meshes and exact control maximization reproduces the worked parameters r=.1,G=.04,k=0,alpha=.3,m0=.35,lambda=.06979961090034 calibrated to v0=10. On 16k mesh, weights at relative share z=.01,.1,.5 are 9.0813254,7.2461777,5.3913987, matching rounded manuscript values. At z=.8,.9,.95,.99,.999 they are 5.0835395,5.1421528,5.2520686,5.4955637,5.6969035. Hence not globally decreasing. The saturation baseline is b/rho=5.8333333. The bounded smooth solution's gain over baseline linear value is positive of order h^(1/(1-alpha)); differentiating gives a negative correction to its slope, approaching the baseline from below. This explains the turn without relying on a grid artifact.

Strongest defense: the tabulated 1%,10%,50% comparisons are correct, and practical stage ranking explicitly says 'for a firm whose decision weight falls with share'. That conditional statement is valid. The unqualified worked-example sentence and any implication of global monotonicity should be limited to the tabulated interval. The dynamic theorem itself does not claim monotonicity and is not refuted. Smallest correction: say 'falls over the reported 1%-50% range'; show full profile or state the late-share reversal. Acceptance test: mesh-converged profile over the entire share interval and terminal asymptotic; rerun any practical claim that uses monotonicity beyond 50%.
