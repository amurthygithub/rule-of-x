## Tests against the strongest defenses

**M1: heterogeneity is not a loophole in this counterexample.** Main23 explicitly posits a common frontier with firms acting on different weights before introducing the separate common-X Assumption5. Supplement7 explicitly says dense growth observations make the cone slope approximate the frontier slope. Thus a heterogeneous-weight example addresses the paragraph as written. It would not refute Proposition3, and must never be presented as doing so. The exact transformation is `q(g)=F^-1(1-chi+chi F(g))`, `Psi_chi=m*(q)`, so `Psi_chi'=m*'(q) chi f(g)/f(q)`. Positive interior density does not force either q close to g or its derivative close to one. My independent standard-library calculation recovers the example's cone LS slope -0.219999999999998, maximum support weight3, inferred maximum .8474074074, maximum spend .54, and FOC residual1.11e-15. Physical s=.25, cap=.5 and lambda=2ell satisfy the strict physical-cap condition. The distribution is intentionally constructed, not an estimate of actual firms. A near-identity quantile map or chi approaching1 under derivative regularity could rescue an approximation, but sampling density alone cannot. Decisive repair: derive through F or remove the positive inference, while retaining the correct descriptive statistic and non-identification result.

**M2: the PV defense succeeds for valuation but not cash timing.** The proof at supplement19-20 explicitly calls the shield a PV and the main28 calls the result an after-tax FCF margin. These are distinguishable claims. With actual vintage deductions D_t=sum w_a I_(t-a), all the taxes can remain linear/full-offset while cash at the investment date still differs from a PV allocation. In a fully mature balanced-growth vintage history using the paper's forward-investment convention, the factor is sum w_a(1+g)^(-a). My calculation reproduces .2505628605 at g=.30 versus .5324255654 at r=.10; the paper's PV-equivalent score exceeds the actual period score by2.28308791 points for k1.5,tau.18. It is below actual by .23874894 points at g.05. The sign is not universally upward. Important equality cases include immediate expensing, no capital exposure, genuinely no deductions, and a fully matched mature history with g=r for this schedule; startup vintages can break that last equality. A finite-horizon PV reduction also needs consistent terminal tax-basis treatment. Do not say the tax law itself is wrong or that all tax-adjusted results fail. Decisive repair: retain actual vintage states or rename the measure and regenerate only affected delayed-deduction, positive-capital cash extensions.
