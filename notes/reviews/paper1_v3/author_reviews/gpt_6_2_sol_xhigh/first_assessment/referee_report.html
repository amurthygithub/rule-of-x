A. Editorial assessment

Recommendation: MAJOR REVISION for an interdisciplinary economics, finance and technology working paper. This is a promising analytical synthesis, with a useful measurement agenda, rather than an identified estimate of the economic limits of AI. The core static frontier, support-score optimization, finite-share score budget, and conditional dynamic verification argument survive the checks performed. Several consequential bridges between those results and measured annual scores or practical recommendations need correction. The empirical tables remain unreplicated because the supplied repository was inaccessible in this review.

The defensible contribution is the score-specific organization of a concave customer-acquisition technology, separation of screening weights from decision weights, a finite budget for excess score within one market, explicit distinction between possibilities and chosen outcomes, and integration of accounting costs and implementation constraints. These ingredients make the Rule of 40 easier to interpret and harder to misuse. Their value does not depend on the numerical scenario being a forecast or on every theorem being unprecedented.

For a theory journal, conditional verification is legitimate, but an existence/regularity result or a sharply delimited theorem, a complete local asymptotic treatment, and a closer priority comparison would be expected. For empirical economics/finance, historical population construction, reproducible filing vintages, a correct estimand-to-technology map, and dependence-aware inference are essential; no causal AI effect or valuation/return prediction is identified here. For a practitioner or interdisciplinary outlet, the synthesis can be useful after measurement repairs, with scenario labels attached wherever numbers are quoted. A publication decision should not rest on agent agreement or the amount of computation.

The principal required repairs are: remove the erroneous common-frontier slope inference; distinguish a tax-PV-equivalent score from period cash flow; supply a consistent annual acquisition bridge; replace the cash-payback-to-owner-rejection implication with a continuation-value calculation; and strengthen the provenance and uncertainty treatment of the execution/cost scenarios. These are distinct from the narrower proof and wording corrections below. The paper's repeated caveats are substantive strengths and materially narrow this report's objections.

C. Strongest reconstruction and claim dependencies

At a fixed share, the firm buys excess growth through g = G + ell sigma^alpha, ell = lambda(1-s/sbar), with 0 < alpha < 1. Growth spend lowers current cash margin; installed capital proportional to revenue adds the charge kg, giving m = m0 - sigma - kg. Inverting the technology yields the concave frontier m*(g) = m0 - kg - ell^(-1/alpha)(g-G)^(1/alpha), for g >= G. A Rule-of-X score Xg+m is a support line, not an equal-value line.

Let y = X-k and C_alpha = (1-alpha) alpha^(alpha/(1-alpha)). With a slack financing cap, y > 0 gives sigma* = (alpha ell y)^(1/(1-alpha)), Delta* = ell(sigma*)^alpha, and R*_X = m0 + yG + C_alpha(ell y)^(1/(1-alpha)); for y <= 0 the optimum spends zero. A binding cap truncates spend. Saturation makes ell vanish and removes the excess-growth surplus. Because d log s = (g-G)dt and R_X-R_infinity <= (X-k)(g-G), the time integral of positive excess score has a finite headroom budget in a single market with the stated no-share-loss baseline. That is a bound on surplus over R_infinity, not an absolute cap on every possible score.

A dynamic firm values future revenue. Under the stated verification hypothesis H, its own instantaneous support weight is v'(s), the marginal value of revenue. That is a different perturbation from a valuation screen comparing persistent growth and margin differences across firms. The latter weight depends on persistence/duration. The worked values are illustrations, not estimates of the weight firms use.

Individual possibilities, an envelope of possibilities, an envelope of observed choices, a pooled dominance-cone quantile, and a local conditional quantile are different objects. The paper correctly proves broad observational equivalence: heterogeneous opportunities/intercepts and optimization can generate flat or upward chosen loci even when individual frontiers slope down. Its surviving error is a narrower positive mapping from a cone-quantile slope to a common technology, not that non-identification theorem.

Automation changes costs, growth opportunities, capital and prices. A fixed-price full-automation cost structure implies an accounting ceiling for the saturated score. Its 32.5% growth threshold follows from selected cost shares, taxes and k=0; it is conditional arithmetic, not an estimated universal constraint. Under the specified homogeneous Cournot/free-entry setup, replicable savings are competed away; scarce-input rents and normal capital/customer-capital returns remain. Implementation then restricts how much of a technically feasible program can embed by a date, given capacity, retries, lags, freezes and persistent realized benefits.

D. Major findings and strongest author defenses

M1. A dense sample does not turn the dominance-cone slope into the common technology's slope

Location: main p. 23, opening of section 3.7; supplement p. 7, S2.3. Classification: demonstrated error in an inference/approximation, with a missing distributional hypothesis. The formal non-identification result is not refuted.

For strictly decreasing common m* and continuous growth CDF F, the exact population cone statistic is Psi_chi(g) = m*(q(g)), with q(g) = F^(-1)(1-chi+chi F(g)). Its derivative is m*'(q(g)) chi f(g)/f(q(g)). Dense support removes sampling gaps; it does not remove the shifted evaluation point or density ratio. A least-squares coefficient of Psi_chi is consequently not the coefficient of m* on the same growth grid.

An exact counterexample to the paragraph's explicitly heterogeneous-weight common-frontier alternative uses alpha=.3, G=k=0, m0=.8, ell=.721826999314566, financing cap 1 and W=.6. Let all choices lie on m*(g)=.8-(g/ell)^(10/3), with chosen weights X(g)=(10/3)g^(7/3)/ell^(10/3). They are score-optimal, spend at most .54, and have margins between .26 and .8. The weights range up to 3. Give growth the continuous full-support distribution F(g)=(g/.6)^14.833990914027952. With chi=.9, the exact cone projection on the author's 0,.05,...,.60 grid is -.22. The true frontier reaches slope -3. Applying the manuscript's approximate multiplier to the cone slope gives only -.8474. Positive density at all interior growth points does not rescue the inference. The effective ell can be represented at an admissible physical share/cap, for example s=.25, sbar=.5 and lambda=2ell; the example concerns the paragraph's common-frontier distribution of choices, not Proposition 3's separate common-X assumptions.

Independent evidence: 101 constrained optimizations confirm the constructed choices to maximum objective discrepancy 6.66e-16; four derivative checks agree within 6.74e-11; increasingly dense weighted support grids converge to -.22. The analytic transformation, rather than those numerical tests, establishes the error.

Strongest defense: the paper itself correctly states the conditional-growth-quantile transformation and proves non-identification. The approximation could hold under a near-identity quantile map, or an appropriate chi->1 limit and regularity assumptions. Neither follows from density alone at fixed chi=.9. Disposition: retain the descriptive slopes and Proposition 3; withdraw the asserted common-frontier parameter/maximum-weight inference and the claim that high decision weights force the other selection story. Practical magnitude in the example is a3.54-fold understatement of the steepest slope.

Smallest repair: delete that identification paragraph or derive the projection through F and state bounds controlling both q(g)-g and q'(g)-1. Acceptance test: the revised procedure must not infer .847 as the maximum support weight in this exact population, and should be checked under uniform and strongly uneven growth distributions.

M2. A present-value tax shield is generally not the period's cash tax shield

Location: main Remark 2, pp. 27-28, equation 27 and ensuing FCF substitution; complete supplementary proof pp. 19-20; positive-k delayed-deduction extensions of Corollary 4. Classification: demonstrated timing error in the claimed general cash-margin interpretation; valid valuation transformation.

For one dollar invested today and deducted one year later at tax rate.20 and discount rate.10, actual cash is -1 today and +.20 next year. Its PV is -.8181818. Substituting psi=1/1.1 gives the correct PV-equivalent initial cost but does not turn today's cash outlay into -.8181818. Full loss offset removes nonlinear tax constraints; it does not advance deduction dates.

For capital vintages with deduction weights w_a, actual D_t = sum_a w_a I_(t-a). Under balanced simple growth g and I_t/S_t=kg in the paper's lead-installation convention, D_t/S_t = kg sum_a w_a(1+g)^(-a), rather than kg sum_a w_a(1+r)^(-a). Applying the paper's half-year 15-year schedule with g=.30, k=1.5, tau=.18, r=.10 gives .250563 versus the PV factor .532426, a2.283-point difference in the modeled period cash score. At g=.05 the difference reverses sign; this is not a one-direction correction. Starting vintages matter as well.

Strongest defense: psi is explicitly defined as a PV, and the proof labels the tax shield accordingly. Allocating each vintage's future tax deductions to its purchase date is a legitimate valuation reduction. Immediate full expensing and genuinely nondeductible capital avoid the timing difference. A fully matched mature vintage history with g=r also makes the two factors coincide for this schedule; startup vintages can break that equality, and finite-horizon valuation needs consistent terminal tax-basis treatment. Disposition: preserve the valuation identity and untaxed theory; correct the period-cash label and any delayed-deduction annual score extension. The headline k=0 threshold and the psi=0 table arithmetic are unaffected.

Smallest repair: call this a tax-PV-equivalent score, or introduce a tax-basis/vintage state and calculate actual cash deductions. Acceptance test: show both equal total discounted value and unequal period margins in startup and balanced-growth examples, then regenerate affected positive-k delayed-deduction tables.

M3. The acquisition algebra is exact instantaneously, but the finite-year bridge changes denominators

Location: main Definition 5 and Proposition 8(a)-(b), p. 48; interpretation pp. 49-50; supplementary S4 proof p. 26. Classification: definition/measurement ambiguity requiring repair, not a refutation of the instantaneous theorem.

The current-revenue-normalized purchased run-rate flow initially defined as g_acq makes C/S=k_acq g_acq coherent in continuous time. Definition 5 then uses the same symbol for log acquired growth ln(1+S_ac/S_cn) and discusses the simple annual component S_ac/S_prior. Neither is generally the same current-normalized flow.

Take prior revenue 100, continuing revenue 100, recognized acquired revenue 100, current revenue 200 and cash consideration 300. The price per dollar of recognized acquired revenue is 3; the actual cash-margin charge is C/S_current=1.5. Multiplying 3 by the simple acquired growth component 1 gives 3; multiplying by ln 2 gives 2.07944. The correct simple annual bridge is k_acq g_acq_simple/(1+g_total)=1.5. A multiple quoted on acquired annual run-rate needs a further recognized-revenue/close-date conversion.

Strongest defense: the proposition's instantaneous algebra is exact; its acquisition technology is separable and explicitly assumes no change in incumbent growth opportunities. The text warns about partial-year recognition, and Table 3 actually deducts measured C/S, so its central arithmetic is not refuted by this example. Disposition: narrow the finding to the annual interpretation and associated universal one-times-revenue hurdle. Do not silently carry the instantaneous net-weight rule into annual measured scores.

Smallest repair: use separate symbols for instantaneous flow, annual recognized contribution, log decomposition and simple annual decomposition; derive the actual C/S charge and specify what is held fixed in an annual decision comparison. Acceptance test: reconcile the example, a late-year deal and organic decline; use a business-level bridge before identifying the entire difference between reported and company-defined organic growth with Definition 5 acquired growth. The paper already discloses that its case company's organic definition differs.

M4. Negative cash payback before exit does not establish that a short-horizon owner rejects investment

Location: main p. 5 and section 7.3 p. 61, with related interpretation p. 66. Classification: unsupported practical implication; missing continuation/exit-value assumption.

The cash-payback statistic is useful, but a holder selling at H receives both pre-exit cash and sale value: NPV_H = integral_0^H exp(-rt) DeltaFCF_t dt + exp(-rH) DeltaV_H. A negative first term does not decide the sign of the total. As an explicit example, pay 1 now and receive a permanent .2 annual flow from t=6 onward at r=.10. Cash PV through a five-year exit is -1. A buyer capitalizing the residual flow gives a terminal value 1.809675 at year 5 and total NPV+.097623, equal to the full-horizon NPV.

Strongest defense: an owner can impose a strict realized-cash payback mandate, receive no credit for unfinished programs, or lack a market for continuing value. Supplement S2.11 p. 10 explicitly discusses owners admitting only projects that pay within their rate and horizon. That is an additional mandate, not a consequence of a five-year holding period. Disposition: the 7.5/8.5-year payback figures are not disproved, but the would-not-fund implication is too strong.

Smallest repair: state that the program fails a five-year cash-payback requirement absent terminal-value recovery. Acceptance test: include an exit/continuation term and identify the terminal-value haircut or financing constraint at which a short-horizon holder rejects the program. This review has not re-estimated the manuscript's payback path.

M5. The central execution path is an illustrative transport of heterogeneous surveys, not a calibrated probability law

Location: main pp. 59-61; supplement S2.7-S2.9 pp. 8-9, Table S11 pp. 36-39 and Table S16 p. 52. Classification: parameter-source transport and unsupported precision/scope; not a false theorem merely because inputs are assumed.

The original BCG report classifies 30% of transformations as meeting targets with sustainable change,44% as creating some value with limited long-term change, and 26% as creating limited value below half the target with no sustainable change. McKinsey's67% realized benefit applies to respondents classifying their transformations as successful;37% applies to the others. Assigning a roughly 70% embedding probability and 67% conditional benefit to repeatable, durable process-level attempts combines distinct populations and success definitions. BCG's woe category is not literally zero value, although the manuscript's like-for-like sensitivity describes it that way. The WEF's59 workers needing training by 2030 measures expected training demand, not a maximum annual rate at which work can migrate, and does not establish a one-for-one mapping from employees to process benefits. Primary sources: Forth et al.(2020), How Winners Win; Bucy et al.(2021), value-at-stake and survey-method paragraphs; WEF(2025), key findings.

Other transports also need common units. Kwa et al.'s low compute costs condition on successful software/ML runs and exclude failed attempts, verification and enterprise integration. They do not identify compute as a share of firm revenue. Asgari et al.'s1.47% hallucinations use 12,999 output note sentences, while 3.45% omissions use 49,590 input transcript sentences. There is no common-denominator conversion in the supplied text from these measurements to the used enterprise rate. Adding them would not be a common-unit calculation; this report does not allege that the inaccessible author code literally performed that addition. The clinical study uses direct-prompted GPT-4 across 18 configurations of 25 transcripts, rather than 450 independent encounters, so the description of all these inputs as retrieval-grounded also needs correction. Legal query-level hallucination rates, summary-level rates and operating-loss intensities are different quantities. Kim et al.'s agreement on the same wrong answer conditional on both being wrong does not identify the probability of a second failure given the first, or a financial-control common-mode hazard. The supplement correctly recognizes that last distinction. These observations restrict interpretation; they do not imply that a particular scenario parameter is numerically false.

Strongest defense: Table S11 grades sources, repeatedly labels analogies and scenario values, and S2.9 reports alternative yields leading to 39.9%,31.5% or 17.4% realized benefits in 2036. The central calibration also correctly removes a separate disruption haircut when survey outcomes already include disruptions. Table 5 explicitly identifies 54-62 as anchor-sampling uncertainty with the program/target held fixed. Those defenses are accepted. Disposition: the path remains a coherent conditional illustration, but its central values are not estimated execution probabilities and the sampling interval is not an uncertainty interval for the path as a whole. The source correctness problem in the BCG zero-value wording is narrower than the general transport issue. The used enterprise error rate should be classed as a scenario unless a dimensional and task-weighted conversion is supplied; an accurately measured source rate does not make that conversion class a. Likewise the 1-60% compute range is a declared scenario domain rather than a demonstrated empirical bound.

Practical magnitude: the manuscript's own alternative yield readings move 2036 realization from about 41% to 31.5% or 17.4%, with the win-only score about 50; its execution scenarios move the score 45-75. Those changes exceed the anchor-only 54-62 band. No reviewed source identifies the firm's full-automation compute share at 6% or an enterprise-wide capability gate.

Smallest repair: retain the scenario, label the probability/benefit/capacity mappings as assumed, correct category wording and error-rate units, and present a joint scenario envelope alongside the conditional anchor interval. Acceptance test: a process-level dataset defining attempt starts, failures, retries, durable embedding, realized savings, capability readiness and all-in costs, or an explicit decision to keep every unvalidated mapping as a sensitivity parameter. Model source error counts on a shared activity denominator before converting to loss shares. Reproduce the METR run-data calculation with failed-run and reliability-cost variants if it is used as a quantitative bound.

M6. Replication and inferential scope remain unresolved

Location: main sections 4-5, Table 2 pp. 40-41; supplementary S6 pp. 28-43. Classification: unresolved replication question and missing inferential assumptions. This is an evidence barrier to acceptance of numerical claims, not a demonstrated extraction error.

No author code or panel was executed. The detailed construction/seeds/sensitivity tables are useful, but independent verification still needs the underlying facts, membership decisions and result generation. Current SIC and a latest-filed revenue screen use later information; partial business/listed-equity checks and noncash acquisitions limit historical coverage. E2 removes most acquisition-affected observations and retains consolidated margins for adjusted organic growth. These are disclosed restrictions. They do not establish any named observation is wrong, and the four views share most data rather than forming independent replications.

The firm-cluster bootstrap preserves within-firm history but not uncertainty over calendar-level common shocks. It can be appropriate for a cross-sectional superpopulation conditional on the realized years; a stronger historical-process claim needs additional dependence analysis. The highest E2 primary cone has 37 firm-years, approximately 3.7 nominal observations above a90th percentile and potentially fewer independent firms. The anchor has 13 firm-years from 7 firms. More than 999 draws would help evaluate borderline corrected p-values such as.097; a simple binomial approximation gives Monte Carlo standard error about.009, an illustration rather than a certified error bar for the stepdown implementation.

Strongest defense: the paper discloses thin support, undefined draws, relaxed draw eligibility and strict-rule sensitivity, withholds unsupported cells, reports method-sensitive significance, and withdraws several structural interpretations. Those are good practices. Ordinary bootstrap inference is not automatically invalid just because a statistic is called a frontier: fixed partial quantiles differ from extreme FDH maxima. Disposition: demand a clear conditional-on-years estimand and executable resampling specification, not a blanket claim that every interval is wrong.

Smallest repair and acceptance test: provide the frozen commit, environment and data described in section G; reconcile every table and numerical macro; report unique tail firms, leave-one-firm sensitivity, draw eligibility/undefined-draw handling, hypothesis families and pre/post-result decisions. State failure to reject rather than no change unless an equivalence margin was tested. Current GAAP decline support at 10% under most procedures and cash-basis failure under all should remain visible.

F. Empirical and financial reproduction record

What was reproduced independently: 250 constrained static optimizations including zero-spend and cap endpoints; two symbolic envelope identities; a separate dynamic solver with mesh refinement; an exact common-frontier population and finite-difference checks; tax-discount factors and vintage examples; economic-profit/ROIC/value algebra; selected Bending Spoons filing ratios and acquisition charge conventions; control-design enumeration; security-cost minimization; capped-tail expectation algebra; fluid execution recurrences; and a random-freeze survival calculation. None imports author verification code.

What was not reproduced: the SEC panel, historical universe, first-filing extraction, organic adjustments, estimation tables, bootstrap draws/corrections, chosen anchor membership from raw facts, calibrate_horizons.py outputs, all numerical macros, dated transition/payback path, or all acquisition capital vintages. No synthetic panel was substituted. Independent examples establish logical issues and validate formulas; they do not re-estimate empirical effects.

The primary empirical estimand Psi_chi(g)=Q_chi(m|growth>=g) is a pooled changing-set quantile. Its LS projection over 13 thresholds is a description of chosen observations. A local quantile-regression coefficient is another projection. Neither is automatically a technological marginal cost. An orientation objection was tested and withdrawn: with x=1-m as cost and y=g as output on the empirical domain, the cited input-oriented conditional-minimum construction can be adapted to two desirable outcomes. This does not import its identification or iid inference automatically. Fixed partial quantiles need not be monotone without further stochastic ordering; the manuscript recognizes this.

H. Narrow corrections, accepted defenses and uncertainty

The local HJB discussion on supplement p. 16 omits explicit share forcing. With z=s/sbar, u=v/s, rho=r-G and a=rho/Delta0-1, its linearization includes z d(u-v0)/dz = a(u-v0)+(v0-k)z+o(|u-v0|+z). The expansion includes C z^a+(v0-k)z/(1-a) for a!=1, with resonance z log z at a=1. When a>1 the forced linear term dominates. This corrects a supplementary asymptotic assertion, not Proposition 2's stated limits. An alpha=.5,lambda=.2,r=.1,G=0,m0=.1,k=0 example gives a3.43649 and linear coefficient-.462557. The original numerical metadata normalized the share cap to 1; a post-freeze check rescales to physical sbar=.5, leaving all dimensionless calculations and v'(s) unchanged and satisfying Assumption 1.

In the worked dynamic example, the reported 9.1,7.2,5.4 weights at 1%,10%,50% of cap check out. The full profile reaches about 5.08 near 80% and rises toward 5.8333 near saturation. Replace unqualified falls-with-share wording with the range actually shown. Practical claims explicitly conditional on a declining weight survive. The exact finite capital-substitution iff objection was narrowed: Corollary 3 already announces first-order results and Corollary 2 supplies a finite-change bracket; repeat the qualifier rather than count a theorem refutation.

The geometric tail of the divisible-program recurrence requires care. With no disruption, U_(w+1)=U_w-p min(c_w,U_w). Once0<U_w<c_w, U shrinks by 1-p and remains strictly positive at every finite wave for 0<p<1. Finite integer programs can finish almost surely in finite random time; an ideal fluid program does not exactly finish at a finite deterministic date. Claims of all embedded by 2032 or completion in 2042 should specify a near-completion threshold/rounding convention and retain residual transition costs consistently. The effect on rounded scores can be small; this does not refute the earlier upper-bound/equality claims or the central pre-saturation calculation.

Proposition 1 constrains excess above R_infinity. Without an upper restriction onG, an absolute Rule of 1000 impossibility is not a theorem: with G9.5,m0=.5,k=0,X1,sbar=.5,s0=.1,sigma=0, the score is 10 forever. This economically extreme example satisfies the static/share assumptions but lies far outside the paper's scenarios. State that at the market-growth rates considered, the target exceeds the saturated score. Do not inflate this scope correction into a separate major economic defect. Likewise the conclusion's necessary-sounding near-complete automation/negligible-capital language should be tied to its stated scenarios rather than asserted over the full parameter domain.

Confidence is limited for empirical estimates, selection magnitudes, bootstrap implementation and dated calibration because raw data/code were inaccessible.
