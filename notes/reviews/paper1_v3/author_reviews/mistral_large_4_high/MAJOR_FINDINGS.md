## F-1. The dynamic value result rests on an unproved verification hypothesis (H) — correctly disclosed, but it is the load-bearing conditional of the paper's "firm's own weight" claim
- **Location:** Prop. prop:dynamic (b)-(c), main PDF pp. 21-23, eq. (18); proof discussion in supp_proofs.tex ("The HJB equation as an ODE"); assumption stated explicitly in model.tex after (18): "We have not proved (H)."
- **Classification:** missing hypothesis (disclosed) — conditional result, not an error.
- **Evidence:** The HJB reduces to a singular first-order ODE in u=v/s with a singular point at (0, v0) where dPhi/du=(r-G)/Delta0>1 (author's own check 28h), so the boundary condition v/s->v0 does not select a solution; existence of a bounded C^1 solution through the terminal singular point at sbar is unproved. My independent collocation attempts either returned the no-spend solution or diverged, consistent with the singularity being a genuine numerical obstacle, not a proof of nonexistence. Author's own dynamic-programming solution (checks 15g-15m, 191/191 PASS) is numerical evidence only, as the paper states.
- **Strongest defense:** The paper never claims (H) is proved; every weight claim is explicitly conditional ("Every statement in this paper that a value-maximizing firm acts on a weight equal to the marginal value of revenue is conditional on (H)"), parts (a),(d),(e) are unconditional and I verified them, and the screening-weight comparison (Lemma 6) does not need (H).
- **Disposition:** survives as a CONDITIONAL result. Not a defect; a stated open problem. The abstract's phrase "Under a verification hypothesis, a value-maximizing firm weights growth at the marginal value of revenue" is accurate.
- **Affected conclusions:** only the decision-weight interpretation (Sec 3.7, exec summary item, discussion); none of the static frontier, budget, ceiling, or empirical results depend on (H).
- **Smallest repair:** none required; optionally add a one-line pointer in the abstract that (H) is unproved (already in body). **Acceptance test for a future proof:** exhibit a bounded C^1 solution of the ODE through sbar for a nonempty open parameter set and verify the feedback generates admissible paths; or prove existence via viscosity theory.

## F-5. The accountability-floor proposition operationalizes a legal standard with a scenario probability ("reasonable possibility" -> pi_bar) and shows a single-provider all-AI design inadmissible in its example — the standard's text sets no number, which the paper states; the example's numbers check out exactly
- **Location:** Prop prop:amin (pp. 29-30), rem:amin-values (supplement S8 / appendix_proofs.tex); AS 2201 A7, Item 308(a)(3).
- **Classification:** stylized model of a pass-fail standard — correctly labeled; no regulator-endorsed threshold is invented (verified in text).
- **Evidence:** P_o formula verified by Monte Carlo; example arithmetic reproduced exactly (3-human pi=4.98% passes 5% "by a hair"; all-AI floor 11.3% > both thresholds; cheapest admissible designs $754,672 at 5% and $804,672 at 1% match the printed AminCenCost/AminSensCost design descriptions). The break-even p^cc=0.000844 and miss-rate 8.4% match author check 25f.
- **Defense:** the paper says the threshold is "a judgment and a scenario value", reports both 5% and 1% side by side, and notes attestation rates (4.6-5.8%) bear on plausibility, not measurement.
- **Disposition:** survives as an operationalization, conditional on the example's common-mode probabilities (0.005 for the AI provider) which are scenario values. One nuance I verified in text: the model defines opportunities as error challenges (N_o=24/yr), so no extra "occurrence factor" is needed — correct as modeled.
- **Repair:** none. **Acceptance test:** estimate p^cm for a real provider under real ITGCs — the number the paper itself names.

## F-6. I could not independently reproduce the numerical HJB profile behind check 15g (v'=9.1/7.2/5.4) with my own solvers; the author's solver is an upwind sweep whose boundary handling at the singular points I did not replicate
- **Location:** code/verify_theory.py solve_hjb (lines 1168-1260); supp_model.tex S2.2.
- **Classification:** unresolved replication question (mine), not a demonstrated error — the paper labels this as numerical evidence for (H) only, and my failure to reproduce it does not contradict (H); it means the numerical evidence is solver-sensitive.
- **Evidence:** my value-iteration (dt-discretized) converged to the no-spend solution v=s(m0-kG)/(r-G) (v/s=5.83 everywhere); my continuous-time collocation ODE from sbar converged to the same. The author's upwind backward sweep from sbar with a terminal condition produces 9.1/7.2/5.4. Singular ODE at both ends admits multiple candidate solutions; boundary selection differs between schemes. Worked-example scalars (lambda for v0=10/15; D0, sigma0, m0; weights vs r table 14.6/10/5.8/3.7/2.1/1.35) I reproduced exactly.
- **Defense:** paper states "That is evidence for (H) at those parameters, not a proof." My result neither confirms nor refutes (H).
- **Disposition:** unresolved; the load-bearing status is already on (H) being unproved (F-1), so this does not change any claim's status. **Repair:** publish a solver-independent check, e.g. verify the HJB residual of the author's grid solution and the closed-loop payoff match (author's 15l does the latter: payoff matches v(s0) within 0.5% — I did not rerun the closed-loop sim). **Acceptance test:** two independent solvers (e.g. their sweep + a viscosity solver) agree on v(s) to stated tolerance on a public parameter set.
