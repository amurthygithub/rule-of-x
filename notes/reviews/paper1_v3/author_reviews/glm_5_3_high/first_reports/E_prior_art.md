## (2) Novelty audit — per claimed-new result (main §2.7, §3 intro)

The paper's claimed-new list (§2.7 p.11, §3 p.11–12): score budget (Prop 1); firm's decision weight vs investor screening weight (Prop 2, Lemma 5); non-identification of firm frontiers from chosen points (Prop 3, S3); accountability floor under common-mode failure (Prop 5); attainable/expected frontiers (Props 6–7); plus the two empirical priority claims ("no prior applications of partial-frontier estimators to the growth-margin plane of software firms", §2.3 p.9; "the only peer-reviewed academic study of the rule is Lee 2024", §1 p.5 and §2.2 p.9).

1. **Score budget (Prop 1, Cor. 1).** *Established ingredients:* market-share saturation ceilings (Marris 1964's demand-growth ceiling is the nearest antecedent: a firm cannot outgrow its market indefinitely); Vidale–Wolfe saturation constants; Bass-type diffusion ceilings; share-point accounting in Lanchester-type models. The paper itself locates its growth technology in that lineage. *New:* the quantitative identity — ∫(R_X−R∞)+ dt ≤ (X−k) ln(s̄/s₀), with the discrete-time analog (12), the sharpness statement (Prop S2), and the "cannot spend the budget twice" corollary. I found no antecedent stating a finite integral budget of score-above-saturation in exchange for log share headroom. **Verdict: plausibly new** (bounded search; Crossref title searches on frontier/budget/share-ceiling combinations surfaced nothing closer than Marris's qualitative ceiling).

2. **Decision weight vs screening weight (Prop 2, Lemma 5).** *Established ingredients:* marginal q (Hayashi 1982 — cited), HJB verification (cited), equity duration (Macaulay 1938; Dechow et al. 2004 — cited), BVP's regression-implied 2–3× (cited, and my verification confirms it), the price-line/tangency reading of Marris–Solow–Odagiri (cited). *New:* (i) the result that a value-maximizing firm's instantaneous objective is exactly a Rule-of-X score whose weight is the marginal q of revenue — **conditional on verification hypothesis (H), which the paper explicitly does not prove** (main p.21, honest); (ii) the screening weight X_scr as a closed-form duration (eqs. 20–21) with its comparative statics; (iii) the two-sided contrast (firm weight exceeds screening weights iff revenue multiple is high). I know of no antecedent deriving a growth-vs-margin exchange rate as an equity-duration object; the nearest relative is BVP's empirical regression, which the paper cites and correctly distinguishes (supp Table S11: "A market-implied screening weight, not a decision weight"). **Verdict: plausibly new**, properly hedged, with the unproven (H) disclosed on its face.

3. **Non-identification of firm frontiers from chosen points (Prop 3, S3).** The paper's own §2.1 (p.7) concedes: "That result is an instance of the identification problem that Marschak and Andrews (1944) raised for production functions." Given that concession, listing "non-identification of firm frontiers from chosen points" among "the results we believe are new" (§2.7) is **internally inconsistent as a novelty claim**: the phenomenon is classical (also: selection/endogeneity in recovered-frontier work; DEA's well-known convexity-of-frontier vs locus-of-optima issues). What is genuinely new is the *worked instance*: quantile frontiers can be strictly increasing even when the possibility envelope is strictly decreasing (Prop 3(ii)), and the observed frontier score is the best firm's tangency score (Prop 3(iii)). **Verdict: novelty overstated as stated; narrowed to "new worked instance of a classical identification problem"** (see Finding E-3).

4. **Accountability floor under common-mode failure (Prop 5).** *Established ingredients:* beta-factor/CCF machinery (Mosleh et al.; Hokstad & Rausand — both cited; bibliographically verified), software CCF (Eckhardt–Lee, Knight–Leveson, Littlewood–Miller — cited and verified), correlated model errors (Kim et al. 2025 — cited), the material-weakness standard (AS 2201 A7; Reg S-K 308(a)(3) — cited verbatim, and I note AS 2201's A7 language matches the quoted "reasonable possibility… not be prevented or detected on a timely basis"). The paper states its own scoping: "What is new is the use of the term to set the composition and cost of a control design that meets the material-weakness standard" (§4, p.29). My searches (Crossref: "AI agents internal control over financial reporting audit", "generative AI auditing…") surfaced a growing AI-auditing literature (e.g., *Auditing Artificial Intelligence*, 2025; *Auditing AI*, 2026 collections; audit data analytics work) but **no formalization of a minimum-cost control-design floor under a common-mode failure term with a material-weakness admissibility constraint**. **Verdict: plausibly new and properly scoped**; the paper should acknowledge the adjacent AI-auditing literature more concretely (see open questions).

5. **Attainable/expected frontiers (Props 6–7).** *Established ingredients:* adoption under learning/capacity constraints (Acemoglu & Lensman 2024 — cited), the productivity J-curve (Brynjolfsson et al. 2021 — cited and used as the analogy, "the J-curve… at the level of a program"), knapsack/greedy arguments (Prop 6(d) is a fractional-knapsack bound — standard OR). *New:* the date-indexed attainable-score frontier under a program clock with disruption risk (reorganizations, leadership changes) and its expectation. I found no antecedent formalizing a "frontier attainable by date t" in the growth–margin plane for automation programs. **Verdict: plausibly new as a formalism** (ingredients standard; the composition is the contribution).

6. **"No prior applications of partial-frontier estimators to the growth-margin plane of software firms" (§2.3 p.9).** Coverage: Crossref searches for DEA/SFA/frontier × software/SaaS firms found (i) **Ge & Huang (2014), IEEE Trans. Eng. Mgmt 61(4):610–622** — stochastic frontier analysis of economies of scale of SaaS firms: prior frontier estimation on SaaS firms, but a *cost/production* frontier, not the growth–margin plane, and not partial-frontier; (ii) marketing-productivity DEA (e.g., Luo & Donthu-type benchmarking, *J. Business Research* 2005) — DEA applied to marketing efficiency, but not software firms' growth–margin plane and not partial frontiers; (iii) a large banking profit-efficiency literature (frontiers in profit space — nearest genre, not software). **No counterexample to the specific claim found.** The claim is hedged ("We have not found"), and my bounded search supports it. Two refinements the authors should consider: acknowledge Ge & Huang (2014) as prior SFA on SaaS firms (it does not contradict the claim but belongs in §2.3), and state the search protocol/date. **Verdict: survives within my coverage; not provable by a bounded search.**

7. **"The only peer-reviewed academic study of the rule is Lee (2024)" (§1 p.5; §2.2 p.9).** **Contradicted as stated.** I found **Nei, Ruus & Indov (2026), "Enterprise Value Dynamics in Publicly Traded SaaS Firms: Revenue Growth, EBITDA Margin, and the Rule of 40 across Revenue-Defined Growth Stages," *Financial Markets, Institutions and Risks* 10(3):130–168** (received 29 Jun 2026, accepted 2 Aug 2026, **published 26 Sep 2026** — i.e., about a month before the paper's stated access dates of 28–29 Oct 2026). It studies 30 SaaS firms 2019–2024 and finds, inter alia, that the Rule of 40 "is better interpreted as a contextual descriptive indicator than as a universal valuation threshold" (Rule-of-40 coefficient insignificant; growth significant). It is **not** a frontier study, so the paper's narrower sentence — "We found no modern peer-reviewed study of the growth–profitability **frontier** of software firms beyond it" — survives. But the unqualified uniqueness claim does not, unless the venue is discounted: *Financial Markets, Institutions and Risks* (ARMG Publishing, ISSN 2521-1242) is a low-visibility outlet whose peer-review rigor may be questioned — that is a defensible author position, but it must then be argued, not left implicit, and the "we are aware of/located" hedge needs a search-protocol statement. **Verdict: narrowed** (see Finding E-1). Note also the Lee sample-size discrepancy (Finding E-2).

8. **Is the novelty positioning against Marris/Solow/Odagiri accurate?** Yes, in my assessment. The paper credits them with the tangency/price-line structure (fair), concedes its growth technology and FOC are standard ("the growth technology is a customer-capital structure in a normalization… the firm's weight on growth in Proposition 2 is the marginal q of revenue of this literature… therefore not new", §2.1 p.7), and claims as new only the specific results audited above. The one overreach is item 3 (non-identification). The demand-ceiling mechanism in Marris (1964) is the honest nearest antecedent of the score budget; the paper's §2.7 phrase "the rest follows from the functional form or is a special case of a standard model" is an appropriately modest frame.

9. **SaaS benchmarking practitioners (consulting: SaaS Capital, Bessemer, OpenView, KeyBanc, a16z, Battery) — do any estimate a growth–margin frontier or trade-off slope?** Within my coverage: **Bessemer does estimate a trade-off slope** — the Rule-of-X multiple regression of FV/NTM revenue on growth and margin (implied ratio ~2–9×, long-run 2–3×; R² 50% vs 62%) — which the paper cites precisely and correctly distinguishes from its own duration-derived screening weight. That is a market-implied exchange rate, **not** an attainable-frontier estimate. Bain (Depeyrot–Heap, Bosche) and McKinsey pieces are descriptive (prevalence and valuation premia); Lee 2024 is a screen evaluation. SaaS Capital's benchmark pages were unreachable (connection failures), but their published products are percentile benchmarks by ARR cohort, not frontier estimates, to my knowledge. **Verdict: no practitioner antecedent found for a frontier estimate; Bessemer's slope is the closest object and is cited.**

**Finding E-1. The "only peer-reviewed academic study of the rule" uniqueness claim is falsified by a second journal study published 26 Sep 2026.**
1. Locator: main §1, printed p.5 (= PDF p.5): "The only peer-reviewed academic study of the rule we are aware of evaluates it as an investment screen (Lee, 2024)." 3. Classification: **unsupported interpretation**.
4. Evidence: Nei, L., Ruus, A., & Indov, M. (2026), "Enterprise Value Dynamics in Publicly Traded SaaS Firms: Revenue Growth, EBITDA Margin, and the Rule of 40 across Revenue-Defined Growth Stages," *Financial Markets, Institutions and Risks* 10(3):130–168, DOI 10.61093/fmir.10(3).130-168.2026; publisher page (armgpublishing.com/journals/fmir/volume-10-issue-3/article-6/) states: Received 29.06.2026, Accepted 02.08.2026, Published 26.09.2026. 6. Verdict: **narrowed**. The claim as stated is not tenable without either (i) a search-protocol statement (queries, databases, date) that excludes Nei et al. on stated criteria, or (ii) explicit acknowledgment and a stated reason for discounting the venue.
