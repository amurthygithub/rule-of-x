## The ICFR treatment, judged in practice

1. **Pass/fail form: right.** A material weakness bars an "effective" conclusion (Item 308(a)(3)), and the test is a reasonable possibility, not an expected-cost comparison. The v2 floor (minimum cost over admissible designs, with the misstatement loss removed from the objective) is the correct reading. The layer model is a reduced form of what auditors do (they test key controls against assertions, not "layers"); say so once.
2. **What "admissible" means is under-defined (N7).** AS 2201 (as read at pcaobus.org) judges severity by the likelihood that controls fail to prevent or detect a misstatement and by magnitude; it "does not depend on whether a misstatement actually occurred". The paper's gloss ("a 5% chance a year of an undetected material misstatement") reads as an unconditional event. Define N_o as misstatement occasions and pi as conditional on them.
3. **Level mismatch in the threshold (N3).** The evidence for 5% is a firm-level rate (the share of attested filers with at least one material weakness); the constraint is applied per control objective, and the example has one objective. Spread across 10 objectives, a firm-level 5% is about 0.5% per objective, closer to the paper's "stricter" 1% than to its central 5%. This reverses the reading that 1% is the sensitivity and 5% the base case.
4. **"An AI agent performing an in-scope step becomes an in-scope application with ITGCs": right as a conservative rule, imprecise as a statement of scope (N10).** Scope follows reliance: a system is in ITGC scope when it performs or supports a key control, a key report or an automated control that the auditor or management relies on. If a downstream human control re-performs the AI's output with precision and tests its completeness and accuracy, the application can stay out of ITGC scope at the cost of that test. Assumption 7 ("each application that performs or supports an in-scope control") is worded correctly; Table S7's "one more in-scope system" is the loose version. ITGCs also give no assurance on output accuracy of a non-deterministic model; the paper handles this correctly (p_j is independent, only p^cm and p^cc fall with theta). Good.
5. **The four channels (process, system, integration, policy).** They appear only in Table 9 (author decision of 10:37). The model prices process (control layers), system (ITGCs: access, change, operations) and integration (interface controls, in the companion's line). It does not price policy (entity-level controls, AI-use policy, management review of AI output) or the third-party channel (model provider as service organization: SOC 1 Type 2, complementary user-entity controls). One mapping sentence in Assumption 7 would fix the reader's problem.
6. **ITGC cost lines (N8, N16).** The $24,000 retest is 10 controls x 16 hours x $150. Only the 16 hours is from KPMG (verified: average testing hours per control for tests of operating effectiveness, FY2024, 146 respondents). The 10 controls and $150 are the authors' own (Table S7 says so; Remark 6 does not). The retest is $19.7k of the $204,672 line (9.6%); the other lines are scenario values. The direction is conservative (the line is 2.6 times the survey-derived $80k reference), so the floor is not understated; "grounded on KPMG" is the overstatement.
7. **The 5% threshold (N4, N5).** Grounded in "adverse attestations of 4.6% (FY2020) and 5.8% (FY2021)". Both numbers verify at Audit Analytics. But later editions exist with different series, the central value was moved from 1% to 5% by a lead-agent decision after results were visible, and the 5% is the lowest round number at which the all-human reference design passes (4.98%). Present it as a judgment (class d), not as precedent (class b).
8. **Robust conclusions, so the paper keeps them:** (a) any design whose common-mode bound exceeds the per-opportunity threshold cannot pass however many layers it stacks; (b) the floor in dollars is small relative to 0.5% of revenue and moves only from 0.090% to about 0.145% of revenue across the sensitivities I ran; (c) ITGC quality and the diversity of the design, not headcount, decide admissibility. What is not robust is which design is cheapest.

## New findings

Severity: **High** = inaccurate or misleading as written, fix before release; **Medium** = fix in the next revision; **Low** = precision or housekeeping.

### N1. High. The cross-type term is zero in every number, so the headline admissible design is knife-edge, and automation bias is unmodeled
- **Location:** Remark 6 p.72 ("there is no cross-type term"); Assumption 7 p.26; Prop. 5(a) p.27; §8.5 p.61. Response row RA-2 says "Fixed".
- **Issue:** The revision added p^cc for management override, defective shared source data or IPE, ITGC failure and "reviewers' acceptance of wrong AI output", then sets it to 0.0 in Remark 6. The 5% threshold gives a per-opportunity ceiling P-bar = 0.002135 (N = 24). The all-human three-layer design sits at 0.002125 (4.98% a year); the one-human-over-one-AI design at 0.001293 (3.1%). Small values of p^cc change which design is cheapest. In the same example the human layer is called "over" the AI layer and is assumed to miss 5% of what the AI misses, independently; that is a review of AI output, the setting in which reviewers accept wrong output.
- **Evidence (my arithmetic, last section):** all-human three layers fails for any p^cc above about 0.00001. One human over one AI layer stays admissible at 5% only up to p^cc of about 0.00084; at p^cc = 0.001 the cheapest design becomes one over two AI ($0.95M), at 0.002 two humans over one AI ($1.40M), and from 0.0021 up no design is admissible at 5% (a_min infinite). At 1%, p^cc above about 0.00014 removes the one-over-two design, and p^cc of 0.0005 (about 1.2% a year) leaves no admissible design. If the human misses 10% of AI-missed items (not 5%), one over one AI is 5.9% a year and fails; at 50% it is 26%, and the cheapest design is two over two AI ($1.45M). The floor in dollars barely moves (0.090% to 0.145% of revenue); the identity of the design does.
- **Suggested change:** (a) Add to Remark 6 a break-even: the largest p^cc, and the largest human miss rate on AI-reviewed items, at which each design stays admissible. (b) State that p^cc = 0 isolates the mechanism, and that the existence of effective-ICFR filers bounds p^cc well below P-bar. (c) Say whether the human layer re-performs the control independently or reviews the AI's output; if it reviews, use a conditional miss rate. (d) Cite the automation-bias literature (round 1: Parasuraman and Manzey, Human Factors 52(3), 2010; not rechecked by this panel). (e) Add: "The example is not a control-design recommendation; admissibility is decided by management's assessment and the auditor's evidence."

### N3. Medium. One control objective, a per-objective threshold, and firm-level evidence
- **Location:** Remark 6 p.72 ("one in-scope control objective"); Prop. 5(b) p.27 (pi_o <= pi-bar for every o); §7.2 p.50; Table S7 p.35.
- **Issue:** The 0.090% design cost and the 0.23% floor are for one objective. A firm has many. The adverse-opinion rate is a firm-level event (at least one material weakness anywhere). Applying 5% per objective is a different, looser standard than 5% for the firm.
- **Evidence:** If a firm-level 5% is split over |O| independent objectives, the per-objective level is 1 - 0.95^(1/|O|): 0.51% at |O| = 10 and 0.26% at |O| = 20. A firm with five objectives each at the central design's 3.1% has a 14.5% annual chance of an undetected misstatement somewhere. KPMG 2025 reports an average of 546 key controls (149 ITGCs) per company, so one objective is a small slice. The "15 applications" in §7.2 counts ITGC lines only, not repeated layered designs.
- **Suggested change:** State the unit ("per control objective") wherever 0.090% and 0.23% appear; say whether pass/fail is per objective or firm-wide; show the floor for |O| = 1, 5, 10, or label 0.23% "one objective". Note that AS 2201.65 aggregates deficiencies on the same account, which is the argument for a per-objective test.

### N4. Medium. The threshold evidence is two editions stale, the series differ, and "grounded by precedent" overstates

### N6. Medium. "No design on one provider passes because common-mode failure is more than remote" is an assumption of the example, not a finding
- **Location:** §7.2 p.50 (unqualified); response summary item 5. §8.5 p.61 and the Introduction are correctly conditional.
- **Issue:** "More than remote" means p^cm = 0.005 against a ceiling of 0.0021; Table S7 calls both common-mode values illustrative. The paper's own Assumption 7 says pinned versions, testing before release and rollback lower p^cm, so a firm with strong change management could pass with AI layers alone.
- **Evidence:** An all-AI design on one provider is admissible only if p^cc + (1 - p^cc) p^cm < 1 - (1 - pi-bar)^(1/N): 0.00213 at 5%, 0.00042 at 1% (N = 24), about 1 in 470 and 1 in 2,400 per opportunity.
- **Suggested change:** Reword §7.2 as in §8.5 ("in the illustrative example"); add the break-even to Remark 6 and say it is the number to estimate.

### N7. Medium. The event behind pi is not defined, and the gloss misstates the standard
- **Location:** Prop. 5 p.26-27; §7.2 p.50 ("a design is admissible if it leaves at most a 5% chance a year of an undetected material misstatement"); Remark 6.
- **Issue:** P_o is "the probability that a material misstatement at one opportunity goes undetected", so pi is conditional on N_o misstatements arising. The prose reads it as the unconditional annual probability that a misstatement occurs and goes undetected. AS 2201's test (the reasonable possibility that controls fail to prevent or detect a misstatement; severity judged by likelihood and magnitude, "not on whether a misstatement actually occurred", as read at pcaobus.org for .62 to .70) is closer to the conditional reading. The two readings differ by the misstatement occurrence rate, and the comparison with adverse-opinion or restatement rates depends on which one is meant.
- **Suggested change:** One sentence in Assumption 7: "N_o counts occasions on which a material misstatement arises; pi is the probability that at least one passes every layer." Rephrase §7.2 and Remark 6 accordingly.

### N8. Medium. The ITGC retest and line are less grounded than "KPMG" suggests
- **Location:** Remark 6 p.72 ("10 controls at the 16 hours per control test reported in a survey of SOX programs (KPMG, 2025), at $150 an hour"); Table S7 p.34 rows "Internal-control cost per in-scope AI application" and "Control retest at each model upgrade".
- **Issue and evidence:** Verified at kpmg.com: 146 respondents, fieldwork April to June 2025, FY2024; average program budget $2.3M (N = 114); 546 key controls, of which 149 ITGCs; 40 in-scope systems; 16 hours per control for tests of operating effectiveness. So only the 16 hours is sourced. Ten controls is 2.7 times the survey's 3.7 ITGCs per system (149/40), and $150 an hour is an assumption; Table S7 says "both our assumptions", the main text does not. The per-year retest is $19.7k (24,000/1.22) or 9.6% of the $204,672 line (access $2k x 15, change $2.5k x 24, operations $55k, interfaces $10k x 4, retest); the rest are scenario values. The line is 3.6 times KPMG's $57.5k per system. A retest after a model change is a re-baselining of a non-deterministic control, not a standard test of operating effectiveness. The external auditor's own testing is excluded.
- **Direction:** conservative for the floor; the issue is labeling.
- **Suggested change:** In Remark 6: "16 hours is the survey average; the 10 controls and $150 an hour are assumptions." Class the line d; show the KPMG range ($16k to $57.5k per system) beside it.

### N9. Medium. External audit effort for AI-performed controls is omitted, and C^acct is design-invariant by assumption

### N12. Medium. B.1 still says agents had "no access to the others' outputs"

## Suggested text

**RA-3, "Use in filings" (for §8.3, after the recommendation to compute on free cash flow after SBC).** Corrected for what I read at the primary sources (Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B) and Regulation G Rule 100 were not in round 1's wording).

> "Use in filings. A score computed on adjusted margins, on free cash flow, on organic growth or with acquisitions charged as capital is a non-GAAP financial measure when an issuer makes it public (Regulation G, Rule 100(a) and (b); Item 10(e) of Regulation S-K). It needs the most directly comparable GAAP measure at equal or greater prominence and a quantitative reconciliation (Item 10(e)(1)(i); C&DI 102.10(a)). It may not eliminate items likely to recur within two years (Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B)) or normal, recurring cash operating expenses (C&DI 100.01), which for a serial acquirer includes acquisition and reorganization costs. Free cash flow is a liquidity measure and may not be shown per share (C&DI 102.07). Palantir presents a Rule-of-40 score as a defined term outside its non-GAAP section, without reconciling the score, though it reconciles the margin it uses. Any Rule-of-X metric discussed in MD&A is also a key performance indicator, for which the Commission expects a clear definition and calculation, why it is useful, how management uses it, the estimates and assumptions behind it and a consistent method, under effective disclosure controls (Release 33-10751). The ceiling, durable score, transient surplus, decision weight and horizon ranges rest on unobserved parameters and scenario values and are not suitable as reported metrics or targets; stated as expectations they are forward-looking statements, for which the statutory safe harbor is unavailable in an initial public offering (Securities Act section 27A(b)(2)(D))."

**Remark 6, break-even sentence (N1, N6).**

> "An all-AI design on one provider is admissible only if p^cc + (1 - p^cc) p^cm < 1 - (1 - pi-bar)^(1/N), which is 0.0021 at 5% and 0.0004 at 1% for N = 24; the example's 0.005 exceeds both. One human over one AI layer stays admissible at 5% only while p^cc is below about 0.0008; the example sets p^cc = 0 to isolate the mechanism, and effective control environments imply it is far below the ceiling."
