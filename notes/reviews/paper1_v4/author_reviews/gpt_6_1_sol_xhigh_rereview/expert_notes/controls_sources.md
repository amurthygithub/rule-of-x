### CS-1 — Major: ownership calibration confuses annualized capital recovery with replacement cash

**Locator:** Supplement p56, Remark S11 (Owning the compute); main Remark 2 pp28–29 gives the relevant cash-versus-PV conventions. Source: Michael and Cottier, Epoch AI, “Servers account for 60% of the total cost of ownership of a one-gigawatt AI data center,” sections Data and Analysis, https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-datacenter-cost-breakdown.

**Demonstrated issue:** The conditional identity k_c=s_srv L_srv TCO is correct only if s_srv TCO is annual cash depreciation/replacement and TCO has the corresponding cash-cost interpretation. The cited Epoch server share instead uses a WACC-based capital-recovery factor, explicitly about0.24 for servers/network. It includes capital's return as well as recovery, and is not1/L. The source states5-year IT life, whereas the paper inserts5.5 years. Source tables show server stock$21,188m, annualized server charge$5,021m and total annualized TCO$8,514m. Thus K_server/TCO=2.4886, while the paper's0.60×5.5=3.3 would imply$28,096.2m server stock. Annual server replacement at the paper's5.5-year life is$3,852.4m,45.25% of the source economic TCO, not60%. The remaining source share includes annualized facility/network assets, so calling all of it running cash also needs reconciliation.

**Consequence:** The displayed16.5%/49.5% and33%/74% markup hurdles are not established by the source calibration. This is not a counterexample to Corollary4 or to algebra under a properly defined replacement-share parameter. The quantitative rent-versus-own interpretation is unsupported until cash cost, invested assets and economic annualization use one basis. Simply replacing3.3 with2.49 and announcing new thresholds would also be incomplete: the denominator and remaining capital-cost components must be rebuilt consistently.

**Proposed fix:** Define rental price B, incremental cash operations O, installed asset stocks K_j, service lives/residual values and replacement schedules directly. Derive steady-state cash and value comparisons separately. If retaining s_srv, explicitly define it as replacement cash divided by cash TCO and estimate that quantity; do not substitute the Epoch annualized share. Recompute all numerical examples and any corresponding code gates.

### CS-2 — Minor: successful-run cost does not alone establish failed-attempt-inclusive cost

**Locator:** main7.2 p59 (compute share paragraph); Supplement S2.8 p9; TableS11 pp39–40; RESPONSE GPT22.

**Issue:** The revenue-unit correction is right:2% of the labor cost times labor60% of revenue gives1.2% of revenue. The remaining phrase “failed attempts included” rests on an additional assumption. A1% cost conditional on successful runs and50% success imply2% per completed task only if failures cost about as much as successes. More generally the long-run cost per success is c_success+[(1-p)/p]c_failure. A benchmark's50%-task-completion horizon is also not the average success probability for every task mix. The public source's Figure14 explicitly conditions on successful runs. This does not make1.2% an impossible scenario; it means the failure-cost and task-mixture assumptions should be stated.

**Proposed fix:** Add that equality-of-attempt-cost approximation, or compute all-attempt spend divided by completed tasks from run data; disclose the task/model mix and include review/integration separately. Treat1.2% as a conditional low scenario, not a source-measured enterprise lower bound. Repository code may contain further evidence, but none was inspected here.

**Source check:** Kwa etal, Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks, NeurIPS2025 paper Figure14: https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/85069585133c4c168c865e65d72e9775-Paper-Conference.pdf (search refturn160search44); abstract https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/85069585133c4c168c865e65d72e9775-Abstract-Conference.html (turn160search2). ### CS-3 — Minor: executive summary omits the one-objective scope of the bottom-up accountability example

**Locator:** main p2, Summary for operators and boards item9, final sentence; Supplement RemarkS10 pp54–55.

**Issue:** The summary reports a “bottom-up accountability floor of a firm with$1billion of revenue” of0.37–0.38%, range0.26–0.76%, without saying that RemarkS10 assumes ONE in-scope control objective and a low-side audit fee. Nearby wording calls the provider-admissibility result illustrative, which reduces the severity, and p55 does correctly state “floor of one control objective.” Nonetheless the executive sentence can be quoted as a firm-wide budget. S2.5 p8 explicitly retains legally required human review/sign-off and p30 states entity/policy/provider controls are unpriced. The example is therefore a partial, conditional construction, not a complete accountability floor for a general enterprise.

**Proposed fix:** Say “For the one-control-objective illustration at$1billion revenue, direct costs plus control layers total…” and explicitly keep the ceiling's separate accountability band. Do not imply that the numerical floor budgets every objective, jurisdiction, provider control and entity-level obligation.
